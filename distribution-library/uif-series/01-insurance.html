Insurance — Your AI Estimate Needs a Fuse, Not a Better Prompt

For: independent adjusting firms, 5–50 adjusters Alt headline: The Line Item Nobody Measured Keyword: AI property damage estimate

Picture the Monday after a hailstorm. An adjuster uploads 140 photos of a roof and siding, and an AI tool drafts the scope in four minutes. It reads clean. It also lists 32 squares of shingles on a roof nobody measured, plus a gutter run that isn't in a single photo. Nothing failed loudly. The draft moves into the estimating platform, the adjuster is on file 19 of the day, and the number ships. Weeks later, a reinspection finds it.

The field that broke: Quantities. Not prose. A clumsy sentence looks sloppy; a wrong quantity costs money, and it's the hardest error to catch because it looks exactly like a real number.

The missing pins: Input context and the fuse. The model was allowed to fill quantities from what a roof usually needs, instead of from a measurement or a photo in the file.

The fix: Every quantity carries its source: a measurement report, a photo ID, or a field note. No source, no line item. Blocked items land in a review queue labeled "no source" instead of disappearing. The adjuster signs off section by section, and the sign-off is logged with the file. Routine claims stay fast; anything above a dollar threshold you set routes to a senior adjuster first. The AI still saves the time. It just can't invent the inventory.

Where else this shows up: Anywhere a model turns photos or paperwork into quantities. A machine shop's quoting tool misreads a tolerance (Manufacturing); a freight parser misreads a rate (Freight). Different documents, same missing fuse: a number that looks real and traces to nothing. If your AI produces a quantity someone pays for, ask which document it points to.
