The big one is Hicksville. New York's own planning/history material identifies the development of the community with Valentine Hicks, who formed a land association in 1834 and helped bring the Long Island Rail Road to the settlement.

There is an interesting historical discrepancy: 19th-century histories often said Hicksville was named for Elias Hicks, while later local historians concluded it was actually Valentine Hicks, Elias's son-in-law. Elias himself had lived nearby in Jericho, which is why the two men became conflated in historical accounts.

But here's where it gets interesting

The Hicks presence on Long Island goes much farther back than Elias Hicks. Historical accounts identify John Hicks of Flushing as one of the early settlers of Hempstead. His family subsequently held substantial property, and his descendants included Elias Hicks, the famous Quaker minister.

One particularly interesting example is what is now Baldwin, Nassau County. It was originally called:

Hicks Neck

The name came from John Hicks, who settled there with John Spragg in the 1640s. The area subsequently went through names including Milburn Corners, Milburn, Bethel, Baldwinsville, and eventually Baldwin.

So if you're tracing the Hicks family geographically, I would not stop at "Hicksville." The emerging geographic chain is:

John Hicks → Hempstead/Flushing → Hicks Neck → Baldwin → Elias Hicks → Valentine Hicks → Hicksville → Long Island Rail Road

And there's another clue: an old U.S. government place-name compilation explicitly records Hicks Island in Long Island's Napeague Bay as named for its owner and records Hicksville as a Hicks-derived name.

One important correction to your wording

I don't currently see evidence that an entire New York county was named "Hicks." The evidence points instead to communities, geographic features, land districts, and historical post offices carrying the Hicks name. There is, however, a Hicks post office/community in Chemung County, documented in the New York State master postal index from 1871-1911.

If what you're actually asking is "map every New York place that was named after a Hicks, including abandoned villages, historical town names, roads, islands, points, necks, and former post offices", that's a much more interesting genealogy/geography investigation. I can dig through the historical place-name records and build you a Hicks New York geographic map + timeline from the 1600s forward, including which Hicks individual each place traces to.

Assistant turn 3

Yes. I was too narrow before. Hicks Neck is only one piece of a much larger Long Island Hicks footprint.

Hicks Neck → Baldwin

  • 1643: John Hicks and John Spragg settled the southward extension of Hempstead.
  • The area became Hick's Neck, named for John Hicks.
  • By the early 1800s it was being called Milburn.
  • 1855: it became Baldwinsville, named for Thomas Baldwin (1795-1872), a sixth-generation Hempstead Baldwin and prominent local merchant.
  • 1871: USPS shortened it to Baldwins because there was already a Baldwinsville elsewhere in New York.
  • 1892: the local government formally adopted Baldwin.
  • Today: Baldwin, Town of Hempstead, Nassau County, NY, including the Baldwin Harbor area.

So Baldwin did not replace Hicks because the Hicks family sold the place or because the Hicks name disappeared. It went through several successive community names as the settlement developed.

One important rabbit hole

There is also Snooksville, in present-day Eastville/Sag Harbor, Suffolk County, which was associated with the Snooks-Hicks family and was subsequently renamed Eastville. That is exactly the sort of thing you were asking for: an old family-associated place name that no longer appears on the modern map under its historical name.

And there is an even bigger issue: the historical record contains Hicks-owned parcels whose names disappeared without the place itself ever being officially named Hicks. Those won't show up in a simple search for "Hicks place names."

If we're doing this properly, the target should therefore be every early Hicks-owned/settled tract, neck, landing, hamlet, village, island, road, estate and community, not merely places containing "Hicks" in the name.
