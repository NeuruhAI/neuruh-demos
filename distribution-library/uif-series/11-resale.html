Resale — The AI Said It Was Rookwood. It Was Not Rookwood.

For: estate sale companies, consignment shops, and high-volume online resellers Alt headline: Your Listing Tool Is Writing Checks Your Inventory Can't Cash Keyword: AI listing generator for resellers

An estate cleanout is 400 items and a weekend. AI listing tools look like a miracle: photograph the item, get a title, description, and suggested price. Then one photo of a matte green vase becomes "Rookwood Arts & Crafts vase, circa 1920." Nobody photographed the base. There is no mark. The tool matched the glaze and the shape and wrote the most valuable answer it could support with vibes. The buyer flips it over, finds nothing, and files an item-not-as-described return. Do that enough times and the problem isn't one refund; it's your seller account. Do it with a designer handbag and the problem is a counterfeit flag.

The fields that broke: Maker, brand, era, and material — every attribution that moves the price.

The missing pins: The fuse and the router. Attribution flowed straight from "looks like" to the listing title, and a high-value claim got the same treatment as a box of kitchen utensils.

The fix: - No mark, no maker. A maker, brand, or material claim needs a cited photo of the mark, hallmark, label, or tag. Without one, the listing says "unmarked" and describes what's visible. - "Style of" is a feature, not a cop-out. Honest descriptive language sells, and it doesn't come back. - Route by value. Items whose attributed value crosses a threshold you set go to a person before listing; designer goods go to authentication. - Photos are part of the record. The listing keeps the photo IDs that back every claim, so a return dispute is a lookup.

The tool still writes 400 listings in a weekend. It just stops promising a Rookwood when you sold a nice green vase.

Where else this shows up: Attribution is a quantity problem in disguise: the claim sets the price, so it needs the same source trace as a dollar figure. Insurance has its own version, a material named on an estimate from a photo that doesn't show it (Insurance). Wherever "looks like" can become "is" on its way to a customer, put a fuse between them.
