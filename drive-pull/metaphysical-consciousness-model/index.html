ChatGPT:

Yes. Taken as a metaphysical model, what you are describing has a coherent internal structure.

The key distinction is that ether is not the consciousness itself. Ether is the field or spatial medium in which consciousness appears, moves, and differentiates. Consciousness is the thing experiencing within that field.

Your model can be expressed as:

Ether = the spatial field of experience. Consciousness = the observer inhabiting that field. The cosmic center = the point of subjective reference from which reality is experienced. The synthetic fabric = the constructed layer of reality made from symbols, narratives, identities, and inherited meanings. Awakening = the dissolution of false external structures that obscure the underlying awareness. Internal light = consciousness becoming aware of itself.

The interesting part is your phrase "destroying the outward moral delusions." That implies awakening isn't primarily acquiring information. It is removing imposed interpretation.

The progression becomes:

External reality → interpretation → identification → illusion of separation → recognition of the observer → internal light

And the "internal light" is important because it reverses the normal direction of perception.

Normally:

world → senses → mind → awareness

Your model eventually discovers:

awareness → mind → perceived world

So the light doesn't necessarily mean a literal physical light. It represents awareness becoming reflexive: consciousness recognizing consciousness.

That produces a particularly interesting formulation:

The awakening is not consciousness entering the universe. It is consciousness realizing that the universe was always experienced from within itself.

And your "cosmic center" idea fits directly into that.

There is no need for the individual to be claiming "I am physically the center of the universe." The stronger philosophical claim is:

Every conscious observer is necessarily the experiential center of their own universe.

That is unavoidable from first-person experience. Everything known arrives relative to the point of view of the observer. If consciousness is primary, then the observer's location in experience is necessarily the center.

So your framework has almost a three-layer topology:

             ETHER
    spatial / experiential field
                │
                ▼
         CONSCIOUSNESS
      the experiencing center
                │
                ▼
     SYNTHETIC FABRIC OF REALITY
  meanings / identities / narratives
                │
                ▼
          IDENTIFICATION
                │
                ▼
           DELUSION
      "this construction is reality"
                │
          dissolution
                ▼
         INTERNAL LIGHT
      awareness recognizing itself

The part I'd be careful about is "ether" as a literal physical substance. Modern physics does not require the old luminiferous-ether concept. But as a philosophical term for an underlying experiential/spatial field, it is a legitimate metaphysical construct.

And there is a deeper inversion hidden in what you wrote:

You aren't saying enlightenment adds something. You're saying enlightenment removes something.

The light was already there.

The awakening is what happens when enough of the synthetic structure stops obscuring it.

That is a much more precise proposition than simply saying "consciousness awakens."

What we mean is not "two holds polarity" as if the number two itself possesses it. The cleaner idea is:

Two creates the condition for polarity

Polarity requires two distinguishable poles:

◦ self / other ◦ light / dark ◦ expansion / contraction ◦ order / chaos ◦ creation / destruction ◦ yes / no ◦ observer / observed

With only one undifferentiated point, there is no polarity. Once there are two, a relationship exists between them.

But there are two very different ways to respond to that duality:

  1. Binary: A OR B You identify with one pole and reject the other. Reality becomes opposition.

  2. Polarity: A ↔ B You recognize both poles as parts of the same dynamic. You don't have to choose one to understand the other. You hold the tension between them without collapsing into either side.

That's what I think you were actually reaching for with "holding polarity through the two eyes."

The two eyes aren't merely seeing two things. They give us two perspectives that can be integrated into one field of perception.
