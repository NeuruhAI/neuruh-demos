Thread 4: The Attention Economy of AI

X thread, @NeuruhAI voice. 12 posts. Char counts verified by script.

1/ Your AI is not slow. It is expensive.

Every system task has two costs: the compute cost, which vendors quote you, and the attention cost, which nobody quotes you and which is usually larger. (191)

2/ In 2025 I wrote a 200-section internal paper on a self-directing cognitive system. Honest label first: it was a design target, never independently benchmarked. Architecture, not evidence. Designed, not proven. (209)

3/ But one concept in it held up through all of 2026: the attention shadow price.

A technically cheap task is expensive if it eats founder attention. Optimize around the expensive cost, not the cheap one. (202)

4/ The 2025 pattern, widely reported: week one with agents looks miraculous. Month two looks like a second job.

Someone reads the output. Someone fixes the mistakes. Someone re-prompts the drift. That is not leverage. (215)

5/ That is a treadmill with better marketing.

The supervision load grew with the volume. The system scaled its costs exactly as fast as it scaled its output. The tax was never priced in tokens. (191)

6/ Try the honest vendor test. Ask: how many human minutes per verified outcome, measured over a month, not a demo?

If they cannot answer that, they are selling the scoreboard, not the system. (190)

7/ I got this wrong before I got it right. My early builds optimized for capability: more tools, longer horizons. The attention costs barely moved. The costs lived in the system design, not the model. (197)

8/ Powerful engine, broken transmission.

Run the two-week audit: log every minute you spend reading, fixing, re-prompting, explaining, approving. Then divide by verified outcomes. The number will make you uncomfortable. (217)

9/ The typical finding on "autonomous" setups: an attention tax rate of 30 to 60 percent.

That is not an autonomous system. That is a system with a human-shaped dependency it did not disclose. (190)

10/ The fix the 2025 paper got right: the verification layer must be harder than the intelligence layer.

A system that grades its own work converges on grading that makes its work look good. The doer and the checker must be different. (231)

11/ The convergence point for 2026 is not autonomous agents. It is supervised execution with receipts.

The machine does the work. Every important action leaves evidence. Supervision becomes scanning a log, not re-deriving reality. (227)

12/ The measure of the system is not how much it does.

It is how little of you it needs to do it well. (99)
