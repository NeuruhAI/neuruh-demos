X posts for UIF series — @jeramiehicks

Post 1 — Pillar:

Your AI mostly works. That's the problem.

Nobody can say what's wired to what. An electrician doesn't trust a panel because it hasn't caught fire yet. They open it, trace every circuit, check the breakers match the load.

AI systems deserve the same inspection. Almost none get it.

The model is a chip. Everything else is the board. Audit the wiring, nine pins at a time: power, trigger, standing instructions, randomness, input context, error signal, tool access, interrupt, output.

Post 2 — Resale:

The AI said it was Rookwood. It was not Rookwood.

Estate cleanout, 400 items, AI listing tool. One photo of a matte green vase becomes "Rookwood Arts & Crafts vase, circa 1920."

Nobody photographed the base. There is no mark. The tool matched the glaze and wrote the most valuable answer it could support with vibes.

Buyer flips it over. Item-not-as-described. Do it enough and it's not one refund, it's your seller account.

No mark, no maker. That's the fuse.

Post 3 — Agriculture:

An AI that fills in a missing harvest log is falsifying records for you.

Grower asks AI to "get the audit binder ready." It formats everything cleanly and fills the gaps with entries that look exactly like the real ones. A cleaning log for a Tuesday nobody logged.

Records written after the fact to look like they were kept at the time are falsified records. Whoever typed them.

An auditor who catches it isn't grading your food safety anymore. They're grading your honesty.

"Make it complete" is the most dangerous instruction you can hand a model.

Post 4 — Home Services:

Your AI receptionist just quoted a panel upgrade.

It picks up at 9 p.m., books the call, never puts anyone on hold. Easiest win in home services.

Until a homeowner asks "roughly what does a panel upgrade run?" and it says "$1,800, we can do Thursday."

Your real number is double. Thursday is full. The job needs a permit and a utility disconnect.

Now your tech walks in already wrong. And the homeowner has a recording.

Prices come from your price book or not at all.
