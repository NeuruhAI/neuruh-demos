14How it works / What this is not / Sources


Read this before relying on any number on this page.


How it works: confirm the park and parcel from at least 2 public sources, count every pad and split park-owned from tenant-owned homes, verify lot rent against a rent roll and a named source, price the infrastructure liability from an inspection or bid, run income to NOI at the stated cap rate, then value the infill upside on fillable pads only and subtract the liability, the infill cost, and the assignment buffer to reach MAO. Every number carries its source or is marked N/A. A share link encodes the whole deal in the URL so someone else can open the same numbers.


What this is not: not an appraisal, not a survey, not legal, tax, or lending advice, and not a replacement for county verification, a title commitment, a utility inspection, a perc test, or a licensed professional. It does not invent pad counts, rents, cap rates, or prices. If a field cannot be verified, it stays N/A. A high MAO does not mean a deal is good, and a PASS does not mean a property is bad. It means the evidence in this run does not support proceeding on the stated exit. No offer is quoted without Jeramie.

14Honest gaps and limits


What this tool does not do, stated plainly.


    

  • It does not pull live county records, rent rolls, or bank statements; every fact is entered by hand and must be checked at its source.
    • 

  • It does not model rent control, local park ordinances, eviction timelines, or tenant relocation costs.
    • 

  • It values lot rent income only; the homes themselves are not appraised here, and park-owned home values are not in MAO.
    • 

  • Cap rate is entered by hand and is only as good as its source.
    • 

  • Infill math assumes the fillable pads can actually be permitted and filled on the stated timeline.
    • 

  • It does not underwrite debt: there is no DSCR, loan-to-value, or amortization math here, so a deal that passes this engine still needs lender underwriting.
    • 

  • It does not check title, zoning, environmental condition, or floodplain status beyond the operator-checked kill boxes. Those need the E8 Distressed Title engine and a licensed professional.
    • 

  • No E7 specification document was found in the vault. Greps for E7 and Mobile Home Park across the vault returned only the UnderwriteX.md line cited in section 14, the 2026-11-04 E7 build itself, and unrelated hits (UUID fragments, drive-lane research files, Lakeshore taxonomy mentions). The Codex V2 Appendix B cited as lineage in the 2026-11-04 build was not located in the vault. What would ground E7 further: the Codex V2 Appendix B document, or a written E7 spec from Jeramie stating the subject and mechanics.
    • 

  • The carried kill 3 contract has an empty-run quirk: infrastructure liability unpriced only fires once NOI exists, so a blank page does not fire kill 3. This is the 2026-11-04 contract carried verbatim. Enter income to leave that state.
    • 

  • Layout was not eyeballed in a real browser. Math was tested in Node with hand-computed values. See the build report.
    • 

  • All sample deals are synthetic and labeled as such.
