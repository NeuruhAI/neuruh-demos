Manufacturing — RFQ to Quote in a Day, Without a Chatbot Touching Your Tolerances

For: CNC and fabrication job shops, 20–200 employees Alt headline: The Extra Zero That Ate Your Margin Keyword: AI RFQ quoting for machine shops

RFQs stack up faster than one estimator can work them. Every day a quote sits, the odds it goes to the shop that answered first go up. So shops try AI: drop in the RFQ and the drawing, get a draft quote back. Here's how it goes wrong. The drawing calls for ±0.0005". The model reads ±0.005". The title block says 7075; the model writes 6061. The anodize note is in a corner the model skipped. The quote is clean, fast, and priced for a part you aren't actually going to make. Win that job and you lose money on every piece.

The fields that broke: Tolerance, material, quantity, and finish — the four fields that set the price.

The missing pins: The router and the fuse. Every RFQ got the same treatment, and nothing forced the critical fields back to the drawing before the price was set.

The fix: - Route by risk. Repeat parts with a quote history get an automated draft. New drawings, tight tolerances, or exotic materials go to the estimator, pre-filled. - Fuse the four fields. Material, tolerance, quantity, and finish must each cite a drawing note or title-block cell. No citation, no quote. - Price by code. The model reads the drawing; your rate tables and cycle-time rules set the number. - One click to confirm. The estimator checks the four highlighted fields, not the whole package.

Your estimator stops retyping and starts estimating. The quotes go out the same day, and the zeros stay where the engineer put them.

Where else this shows up: Routing by risk travels furthest. An adjusting firm sends large losses to a senior adjuster (Insurance); an estate seller sends high-value pieces to a person before they list (Resale). Same router, different threshold. Define what "high stakes" means in your operation, then wire that path before the model ever sees the job.
