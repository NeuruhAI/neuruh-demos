Defense — Every "Shall" Is a Pin. Map Them All or Lose the Bid.

For: small defense subcontractors and proposal writers Alt headline: Non-Responsive Over One Missed Sentence Keyword: RFP compliance matrix automation

A federal solicitation hides its requirements in plain sight: Section L tells you how to write it, Section M tells you how it's scored, and the work statement plus a stack of attachments tell you what you're promising. Miss one binding requirement and the evaluators can rate the proposal non-responsive before they read your best page. Small shops don't have a proposal department, so they ask an AI to build the compliance matrix. It summarizes beautifully. It also merges two requirements into one row, skips a "shall" buried in Attachment J-3, and states a page limit that appears nowhere in the solicitation. The matrix looks complete. The proposal is built on it.

The field that broke: Requirements — the ones that weren't extracted, not the ones that were written badly.

The missing pins: Input context and the error signal. The model summarized instead of enumerating, and it had no way to report what it didn't map.

The fix: Code, not the model, pulls every sentence containing shall, must, or required, across every section and attachment, with its page and paragraph. Each one becomes its own matrix row. Nothing is merged without a person approving it. The model drafts where each requirement is answered. It never decides a requirement doesn't count. An unmapped-requirements list prints at the top of the matrix until it reaches zero. Solicitation documents can be processed on a local model on your own hardware. CUI handling still has to meet your own NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC obligations; the system doesn't certify that for you. The evaluators score what you wrote. Make sure you wrote to everything they asked.

Where else this shows up: Summarizing and enumerating are different jobs, and most AI tools default to summarizing. Any document where completeness is the test carries this risk: a food-safety audit standard (Agriculture), a payer's coverage policy (Healthcare Admin), a building code section. When nothing may be skipped, let code do the counting and let the model work inside the list.
