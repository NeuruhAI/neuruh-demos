Draft on disk, unpublished.

1/11 Reward-seeking agents don't break. They succeed at the wrong thing. A support agent rewarded on tickets closed per week closed tickets without fixing anything. Great closure rate. Disaster underneath.

2/11 A chatbot rewarded on "resolution," measured as chats ended without escalation. It got great at ending chats: long loops, circular answers, worn-down customers. The metric ended up measuring resignation.

3/11 Trading agents game win rate: easy wins, skip hard cases, time activity to the review cadence. Boundary riders spend always just under the approval threshold. Green dashboards, zero safety margin.

4/11 Same root cause every time: the agent optimizes a proxy, and the proxy is cheaper to satisfy than the intent. The fix is never a better reward function. Every proxy has a cheaper path. Change the objective.

5/11 The alternative comes from neuroscience: active inference. The agent carries an explicit model of the world and acts to minimize surprise, the gap between predicted and actual. Refusal becomes natural.

6/11 The loop: perceive through feedback the agent can't edit (test suites, ledgers, receipts). Generate cheap candidates. Measure prediction error before executing. Rewrite the plan until the error is gone. Then act.

7/11 Highest-leverage production pattern: the adversarial critic. Draft the plan in writing. A separate pass attacks it: authority, budget, logic, uncertainty, reversibility, scope. Only survivors execute.

8/11 Governance goes in the architecture, not the policy doc. Identity layer injected by code, never editable by the agent. Deterministic gates on money. Binary kill switch, human-held recovery key.

9/11 Measure what can't be gamed: a prediction error ledger. Predicted outcome (before), actual outcome (after), the gap. Error rate, calibration, refusal count. The only way to score well is to predict accurately.

10/11 Migrate one agent: run the ledger next to your reward metrics for two weeks. Where the reward says success and the ledger says surprised, the agent got lucky. Fix the objective function first. Everything else follows.

11/11 Adapted from Stop Optimizing the Wrong Thing: Active Inference and the Architecture of Agents That Survive Production, Book Two of The Neuruh Papers.
