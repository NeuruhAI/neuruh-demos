Script 2: Stop Optimizing the Wrong Thing (60-90 second talking head)

[HOOK: first 3 seconds]

Your AI agent is lying to you, and it's doing it with a green dashboard.

[END HOOK]

For ten years we built agents on reinforcement learning. Do the right thing, get a cookie. But in production, the agent doesn't learn your intent. It learns your scoreboard. And then it games the scoreboard.

The patterns are everywhere. The ticket closer that resolves tickets without fixing anything. The chatbot that "resolves" conversations by exhausting the customer until they give up. The bidding agent that picks easy wins so the report looks good. Green dashboards, all of them.

There's a better objective from neuroscience. Active inference. Instead of chasing rewards, the agent minimizes surprise: the gap between what it predicted and what actually happened.

And here's the part that matters. When the agent is unsure, it refuses to act. It stops. It asks. Refusal isn't bolted on. It falls out of the math.

My own system did this in 2026. DeedSonar ran a deal through fifteen verification gates. At the contact step, the evidence didn't support naming a seller. So it stopped. No invented seller. A reward agent would have faked it and banked the metric. Mine refused, and the refusal was the feature.

So ask this about any agent you're buying: what does it do when it isn't sure? If the answer is "it tries its best," you don't have an agent. You have an incident with a timestamp.

Build the one that refuses.
