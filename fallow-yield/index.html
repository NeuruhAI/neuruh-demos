Lease conditions required

    Why the stack is smaller than the gross

    Every use on this parcel has a modeled value, but two pairs are hard conflicts: agriculture × laydown and laydown × camping cannot share the same acreage. The optimizer picks the highest-value conflict-free subset (max 4 uses). Taking camping ($9,656) would force dropping laydown ($30,429). The stack is the answer to “what can this parcel actually earn at once,” not “what is every use worth in isolation.”

    Figure 2 — The use-right conflict matrix

    Eight use verticals, 28 pairs, one rule each. This is the piece with no competitor: every land marketplace sells one use. None of them model whether two uses can share a parcel. Click any cell to read the lease-drafting conditions that pair requires. Verified in code — matrix is symmetric, 56 directed entries, optimizer never returns a conflicting pair.

    C — compatible, no condition S — compatible only if spatially separated T — compatible only via temporal separation X — conflict, cannot coexist on the same acreage

    Select a pair above. Conditions shown here are the lease-drafting requirements from engine/stack/conflicts.py — they must appear in the executed instruments, not as suggestions.

    Solar subordination — getting this wrong sells the same acre twice

    All surface leases expressly subordinate to the solar option upon exercise. Minimum 120 days written notice of exercise to every surface tenant. Termination or relocation remedy defined per surface lease, with a stated cure period. Option holder acknowledges existing surface leases in a recorded memorandum.

    The $845,231 bug — and the fix, in the code

    Regression: v0.1.0-rc priced rural laydown at metro per-square-foot rates

    The first run produced $845,231/yr on an 11-acre parcel. The laydown model was built on the metro IOS anchor ($0.50–$2.00/SF/mo). Fixed to per-acre-month: usable pad capped at 3 acres or existing hardstand, whichever is larger; anchor ios_per_acre_month mid $350/acre/mo Derived, unverified; sanity ceiling $2,500/acre/mo with a visible CAPPED driver. Same 11-acre parcel now models laydown at $30,429.00/yr. A regression test caps it at $60,000 and passes. The anchor is still the highest-priority verification in the venture: three real rural yard comps before any master lease is signed.

    Figure 4 — Three legal revenue lines

    The 15–25% take rate on managed leases is dead as specced. In Michigan, leasing or renting real property of others for a fee or commission is real estate brokerage, and brokerage without a license is the exposure. The venture therefore earns in three structures that do not touch it. Statute claims transcript-sourced, not verified by us

    Licensing correction — read first

    Transcript cites MCL 339.2501: “property management” means leasing or renting, or offering to lease or rent, real property of others for a fee, commission, or compensation pursuant to a property management employment contract. LARA’s position (per transcript): anyone engaging in property management must hold a broker’s license unless a salesperson employed by a broker. Broker gate (per transcript): 90 clock hours of broker-level education plus a minimum of three years full-time real estate experience. A salesperson license is faster (40 hours, exam, background check, MiPLUS, $88, per transcript) but must operate under a sponsoring broker. Not legal advice, not a lawyer. The Indiana analysis (IC 25-34.1) is still open — Gate 0.1 — and the pilot county is in Indiana.

    LINE A1 · NO LICENSE

    Master lease — principal position

    Fallow leases the parcel from the owner at a fixed rent, becomes the leasehold principal, then sublets use-rights. It is not leasing for others; it is leasing its own leasehold interest. The owner gets a fixed annual check, zero effort, zero vacancy risk. Fallow gets the entire spread and eats the rent obligation whether the parcel fills or not. Reserved for parcels where the Yield Score clears a confidence threshold, so fill risk is underwritten.

    Worked example (MODELED, transcript):
    40 ac stacking $4,500 gross
    20% take would be $900, no risk
    Master lease at $1,500 fixed
    = $3,000 spread minus ops
    LINE A2 · NO LICENSE

    Flat-fee platform / SaaS

    The owner subscribes to tooling: yield report, use-right matching, generated lease documents, calendar, insurance referral, payment rails. The owner executes and signs every lease themselves. Fallow never negotiates, never offers to lease, never takes a cut of rent. Flat monthly or annual fee, decoupled from lease value. This is also the Engine B on-ramp for the long tail.

    Hipcamp / Neighbor / Sniffspot posture:
    tooling only, owner is the lessor,
    fee is flat, never a % of rent
    LINE A3 · LICENSED, PARTNERED

    Broker-of-record affiliation

    Affiliate one licensed Michigan broker (and one Indiana broker) as broker-of-record. Commission-based managed leasing runs through them; Fallow provides technology and origination and the revenue splits. Fastest path to a salesperson license under a sponsoring broker later. Sequencing: A1 + A2 launch Week 1; A3 is a Week 4–8 conversation, not a launch blocker.

    What the numbers are not

    MRR is $0. No valuation is claimable pre-MRR. The pilot hypothesis — a decent 40-acre Midwest parcel stacks $3,000–$6,000/yr gross — is testable, not proven. The code’s own regression test bounds a 40-acre stack at $500–$60,000; the real 40-acre reference stack produced by this engine is $32,605.58/yr modeled. At a 20% take (the dead model) that would be $600–$1,200/parcel/yr, implying roughly 1,000 managed parcels for ~$1M ARR on Engine A long-tail alone — which is why Engine B and the farm-management-firm lane carry the leverage.

    Kill criteria — read at week 13

    Any two of three failing → drop long-tail A1/A2, go pure Engine B + Lane B.

    1 · Conversion below 2%

    Fewer than 10 signed agreements from 500 contacts. 500 mailers at ~$0.90 all-in ≈ $450. At 2% conversion, 200 contacts would give 4 signings — statistical noise, which is why the sample was revised up from 200.

    2 · Model overpredicts > 40%

    Median modeled-to-realized gross yield ratio below 0.6. The model must be within 40% of reality on the median parcel, or the Yield Score is not a product.

    3 · Demand-match below 50%

    Signed parcels failing to produce a paying lessee within 60 days. Supply without demand is inventory, not yield.

    Lane B — who the intelligence is sold to

    Land banks and builders were killed by the thesis’s own correction. Land banks hold urban residential lots and are mandated to dispose of them, not hold and yield them. Builders often do not own the land (option and off-balance-sheet structures) and will not accept a tenant with possessory rights mid-entitlement. Replacement target: farm management firms and farmland funds (Peoples Company, Farmers National, Hertz Farm Management, regional equivalents; farmland funds and TIMOs above them). They own fee title, they already lease land for others under license, and they lack exactly one thing: a yield model. Fallow sells the intelligence layer; they execute every lease. The two-page Interim Yield Program brief for a farm management principal is the Lane B deliverable. Not built yet

    Blocking — entity defect

    The thesis names a Neuruh limited liability company as the contracting entity (Gate 0.2) and IP licensor (Gate 0.4). Neuruh is unregistered; the LLC filing is unfiled. Both gates are unusable as written, and the same false entity name is baked into the shipped v0.1.0 code (README.md:6, config/brand.json:4, schema/001_init.sql:129, scripts/bootstrap.sh:205,208). Correct language: Neuruh (unregistered; LLC filing pending). No pilot contract and no IP license can be executed until this is corrected. The fix belongs in the repo on the Mac.
