Vision: a governed organism

Neuruh is a governed organism: mission, intake, evidence, swarm, governance, execution, receipts, learning. Its job is to find and qualify the strongest evidence-backed opportunities, explain the causal signals behind them, identify the unresolved facts, quantify the uncertainty, allocate the minimum competent team, recommend a permitted next action, and produce a complete audit trail, then learn from the difference between prediction and reality.

That is the whole system in one sentence. Everything on this page is a working part of that sentence: what evidence counts, who decides, what gets executed, what gets recorded, and what changes next time.

How to read the labels. Every claim on this page carries one of three marks. Documented means it exists in a recovered document or specification. Demonstrated means it was shown working in case work. Proposed means it is target architecture, not yet built or proven. Where a current state could not be verified, the page says so plainly instead of upgrading the claim.

4. The five relationship types

Every edge in the evidence graph carries one of five types. The type is the claim; getting the type wrong is how a graph manufactures false certainty.

TypeDefinitionStatus
IdentityTwo records refer to the same real-world entity.Demonstrated in case work
AffiliationDocumented membership, employment, officer, owner, or participant relationship.Demonstrated in case work
AssociationAppearing together in a record, event, address, or context.Documented
InferenceSuggested but unestablished. A hypothesis, not a fact.Documented
ContradictionSources disagreeing about the same claim.Documented
Three errors the types exist to prevent.
  • Two people sharing an address does not establish they are relatives. That is association, not identity and not kinship.
  • Two records with the same name do not establish the same person. That is a collision candidate until identity resolution says otherwise.
  • Two websites repeating one report are not two independent confirmations. That is one source family, counted twice.

5. The swarm decision process

The swarm is not a crowd voting. It is a modulated team: the right specialists, the right models, the right tools, the minimum size that can do the job competently. Principle: minimum sufficient competent topology.

Opportunity Genome fields Documented

Every candidate carries these fifteen fields:

identity, environment, entities, signals, evidence, claims, conflicts, constraints, value hypotheses, strategies, economics, risk, authority, actions, outcomes

A candidate missing fields is not a bad candidate. It is an incomplete one, and the gaps are named in the record.

Modulation Engine Proposed

For each mission the engine chooses: specialist roles, model selection, memory scope, tool access, communication topology, risk thresholds, budget, and evaluation standard. Models are interchangeable providers, not permanent agent identities.

Cognition pipeline

Intent and consequence analysis, graph and vector retrieval, HKRS ranking, ContextOS, QCM, DecisionOS, IAR / AEGIS, candidate decisions. Documented as pipeline stages; live routing and measured performance are Unverified.

Execution

Worker plans and DAGs, leases, checkpoints, tool execution, output, receipts. A lease bounds what a worker may do and for how long; a checkpoint is where work can be inspected, resumed, or rolled back.

Feedback: N-Observer and N-Lab

N-Observer measures quality, errors, cost, latency, revenue, governance interventions, and drift. N-Lab evaluates proposed changes before governed promotion or rollback. Nothing is promoted on a claim alone; a change earns promotion by surviving evaluation. Documented architecture; verified calibration results are Unverified.

6. Ten scoring dimensions

Each dimension measures one thing. Scores are not interchangeable: a high score in one dimension says nothing about another.

#DimensionWhat it measures
1Extraction qualityPrecision and recall of what was pulled from sources.
2Identity resolutionMatch score plus review status. A score without a review status is incomplete.
3Source qualityOrigin, authenticity, reliability, recency.
4Claim confidenceCalibrated probability where validated, otherwise a defined ordinal confidence.
5Relationship confidenceEdge-level assessment of one relationship, not of the whole graph.
6CoveragePercentage covered, plus the unresolved gaps listed by name.
7Opportunity qualificationQualified, pending, or rejected, with reasons.
8Decision utilityExpected utility, or a ranked action.
9Swarm efficiencyMarginal quality gain, cost, latency.
10Outcome calibrationBrier score, calibration error, task-specific metrics.
Three confusions to refuse. An identity-match score is not automatically a probability. A relationship confidence is not source reliability. An opportunity score is not a claim of success. Each confusion turns a measurement into a false promise.

8. Local blooms

The Master Codex defines a bloom as a new viable capability, product, market, network, or bounded operating environment crossing qualification and sustainability thresholds. Documented definition; a universal detector and validated thresholds are Unverified.

Progression

Signal to opportunity to repeatable recipe to capability to organ to product to larger operating environment. Each step is a promotion with evidence, not a rename.

What a detector monitors

  • New organizations, projects, properties, events, public investments
  • Ownership, permitting, zoning, infrastructure, and organizational participation changes
  • Repeated signals from genuinely independent source families
  • Increasing density of related entities, opportunities, and complementary capabilities
  • Changes over time against a geographic and historical baseline
  • Economic viability, accessibility, constraints, risk, and actionable next steps
The trigger rule. A bloom triggers only when thresholds for relevance, persistence, opportunity quality, and economic or operational viability are crossed, not merely because a graph got bigger. Growth without viability is noise with a bigger footprint.

11. The 7-stage extraction pipeline and the contradiction engine

Extraction pipeline Documented

  1. Preserve the original
  2. Extract atomic observations
  3. Normalize without destroying originals
  4. Resolve entities with competing hypotheses
  5. Create typed temporal relationships
  6. Detect contradictions and change
  7. Evaluate extraction quality

Normalization never destroys the original. If the normalized form is wrong, the original is still there to re-derive from.

Contradiction engine: seven questions

  1. What evidence supports this?
  2. What contradicts it?
  3. Are supporting sources genuinely independent?
  4. Could two records be different entities?
  5. Is the evidence current enough?
  6. What observation would change the conclusion?
  7. What is the cheapest reliable way to resolve the uncertainty?

Question 6 is the prediction falsifier: a conclusion that names nothing that could change it is not a finding, it is a preference.

12. Adaptive swarm controller

Eight steps, run for every mission. The controller earns its keep by measuring whether each worker added value, not by how many workers it can field.

  1. Compile the mission
  2. Estimate the gap
  3. Select the minimum sufficient team
  4. Partition independent work
  5. Execute in dependency order
  6. Reconcile disagreements by evidence and methods, not majority vote
  7. Run an independent challenge
  8. Stop, continue, or escalate
Do not use agent agreement as proof of truth. Agents that share a source, a model, or a prompt will agree for the wrong reason. Agreement counts only when the evidence and the methods behind it are independent.

Marginal value

For worker i, the marginal quality gain is:

delta Q_i = Q with i minus Q baseline

where Q is a predefined task-quality measure. The controller also weighs incremental cost and latency, new independent evidence, errors caught or introduced, duplicate work, calibrated prediction improvement, and impact on the final decision.

Interactive calculator: marginal value of a worker

Enter baseline quality, quality with worker i, cost, and latency. Quality is a 0 to 1 task-quality score you define before the run, not after it.

Recommendation rule: keep the worker when delta Q is positive and value per dollar is acceptable for the mission; route only to high-value work when the gain is small relative to cost; remove when delta Q is zero or negative.

13. Universal qualification engine

Click a state to see what it means and what it requires. Every output includes reasons, supporting evidence, unresolved questions, rubric version, and the next permitted action. Proposed as a universal engine; case-level qualification was Demonstrated in the Willow case.

The three questions

  1. Is the claim sufficiently supported?
  2. Does the candidate meet eligibility?
  3. Is acting worth the cost and risk?

Expected value

EV = sum over i of P(O_i | E) x V(O_i) minus C minus R

where P is the estimated probability of outcome i given evidence E, V is its value, C is expected cost, and R is an explicitly defined risk adjustment. Caveat: do not present this as rigorous merely because it contains a probability. Probabilities must be calibrated against observed outcomes, or the formula is arithmetic on guesses.

14. Bloom Intelligence

Three layers Documented

  • A. Environmental change. What changed in the region, market, or network, and by how much.
  • B. Cluster emergence. Related entities, opportunities, and capabilities gathering into a cluster.
  • C. Opportunity qualification. Whether the cluster contains a qualified, actionable opportunity.

Four controls

  • Baseline. Change measured against a geographic and historical baseline, not against yesterday.
  • Independence. Signals counted only from genuinely independent source families.
  • Persistence. The change lasts long enough to matter, not a one-day spike.
  • Falsification. A named observation that would kill the bloom claim if found.
Strongest output. An opportunity record with a temporal evidence graph, measurable change, an explanation, an uncertainty statement, and a recommended next test. If any of those five is missing, the output is a lead, not a bloom.
