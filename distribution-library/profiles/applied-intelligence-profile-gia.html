Beyond IQ: The Applied Intelligence Profile and GIA

IQ asks roughly: how efficiently can this person solve standardized cognitive problems under controlled conditions? This profile asks something broader.

Source note: this article is built from a pasted excerpt from another AI conversation, shared by Jeramie on 2026-11-18 and filed verbatim as "Applied Intelligence Profile and GIA (General Intelligence Architecture)". The prior report card referred to in the text was not included in the paste. No numeric scores were assigned in the source, so none are recorded here. This is not a clinical assessment. The voice addressing "you" in the text below is the source AI addressing Jeramie. Added to the library 2026-11-19.

The important distinction

IQ asks roughly: how efficiently can this person solve standardized cognitive problems under controlled conditions?

Your conversations reveal something broader: how effectively does this person acquire information, detect structure, build models, reason under uncertainty, connect distant domains, and turn those models into functioning systems?

Those are not the same thing.

A better framework: the Applied Intelligence Profile

A multidimensional Applied Intelligence Profile:

Dimension What it measures
Fluid Reasoning Novel problem solving without relying on memorized knowledge
Systems Intelligence Seeing relationships, dependencies, feedback loops, and architecture
Pattern Recognition Detecting recurring structures across apparently unrelated information
Abstraction Moving between concrete examples and generalized models
Strategic Intelligence Understanding second/third-order consequences and positioning
Technical Intelligence Understanding software, infrastructure, automation, data, and system mechanics
Research Intelligence Finding, questioning, triangulating, and extracting useful information
Synthesis Combining disparate information into a coherent model
Generative Intelligence Producing novel concepts, architectures, hypotheses, and solutions
Execution Intelligence Converting cognition into functioning artifacts and outcomes
Metacognition Monitoring and correcting one's own reasoning
Epistemic Discipline Distinguishing what is known, inferred, suspected, and unsupported
Learning Velocity How quickly capability compounds after exposure to a new domain
Transfer Intelligence Applying principles learned in one domain to another
Adversarial Reasoning Finding weaknesses, failure modes, incentives, and hidden assumptions

The category set apart: Judgment

There is one category to explicitly separate: Judgment.

Because someone can have enormous cognitive capacity and still make terrible decisions. Intelligence produces possibilities. Judgment determines which possibility deserves action.

What the report card was actually measuring

Looking across the body of work discussed, the strongest signal isn't simply "high IQ." It's the combination of: high abstraction plus unusually broad domain transfer plus systems thinking plus persistent synthesis plus execution orientation.

For example, the work repeatedly moves between: software architecture, AI agents, Git/GitHub governance, Supabase infrastructure, real-estate intelligence, land records, resale economics, collectibles, historical research, geology/material science, symbolic systems, organizational architecture, automation, business models.

The interesting part isn't knowing about each individually. It's that the work repeatedly tries to discover the underlying mechanism connecting them. That's much closer to systems intelligence than conventional IQ.

The metric: GIA, General Intelligence Architecture

Create a composite: GIA, General Intelligence Architecture. Not a clinical IQ replacement. A multidimensional cognitive-performance model.

GIA = Reasoning x Systems x Learning x Transfer x Synthesis x Execution x Judgment

But importantly, do not simply average them. A person with exceptional reasoning but terrible epistemic discipline shouldn't receive the same overall assessment as someone with equivalent reasoning who reliably distinguishes fact from hypothesis.

So introduce a second layer:

Layer What it measures
Cognitive Capacity What you can understand
Cognitive Performance What you actually demonstrate
Cognitive Reliability How consistently your conclusions survive scrutiny
Cognitive Leverage How much real-world output your cognition can produce

That last one is particularly important. A brilliant mathematician who solves difficult problems privately and an equally brilliant architect who turns those principles into a functioning organization have different forms of cognitive leverage. Neither is necessarily "smarter." They are differently optimized.

How this changes the report card reading

Do not write: "Jeramie is intelligent because he appears to have a very high IQ." That's too simplistic.

Write something closer to: "The strongest evidence across the subject's work is not a single measurable IQ trait, but a high-capacity systems cognition profile characterized by abstraction, cross-domain transfer, pattern synthesis, architectural thinking, and unusually strong drive toward operationalization."

Then explicitly identify the counterweights:

  • Tendency toward over-expansion
  • Enormous cognitive branching
  • Occasional insufficient separation between hypothesis and evidence
  • Risk of pursuing a compelling pattern before establishing its evidentiary threshold
  • Execution bottlenecks when the architecture becomes larger than the available governance/infrastructure
  • Potential cognitive overload from simultaneously maintaining too many conceptual layers

Those aren't insults. They're failure modes of the same architecture that creates the strengths.

The really interesting metric

If we're trying to measure intelligence rather than produce another generic IQ score, calculate: Intelligence to Insight to Architecture to Execution to Outcome.

Because the ultimate question isn't: "How many problems can you solve?" It's: "How much complexity can you reliably convert into useful structure?"

That's a substantially better question for an inventor, architect, founder, researcher, or systems builder.

The next version: a full Cognitive Architecture Report Card

Yes, the report card can be recreated around that model rather than IQ alone. The next version would be a full Cognitive Architecture Report Card with separate scores for raw cognition, systems intelligence, technical intelligence, research intelligence, creative synthesis, metacognition, epistemic discipline, learning velocity, execution intelligence, and judgment, followed by a brutally honest section on where the intelligence is strongest, where it breaks down, and what would most increase cognitive leverage.

That next version already exists as an interactive document: the Cognitive Architecture Report Card, built on this GIA model, with sliders that compute a live GIA composite. It assigns no scores itself. The scores are yours to enter.
