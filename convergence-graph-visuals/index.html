Where the DAGs converge

Six hubs appear again and again. They are the joints of the graph. Lose one and multiple DAGs fail at once.

Receipt

The universal handoff. Execution, delivery, custody, and proof all require one. A receipt-chain break is a global stop.

DFN

Founder attention boundary

Section J is a design rule, not a preference. The founder is the authority at specific points — and explicitly not the transport layer everywhere else.

Founder input belongs here only

  • ✓ Objective — what is being pursued and why
  • ✓ Consequence — weighing what happens if it is wrong
  • ✓ Joint business authority — decisions that bind the venture
  • ✓ Ambiguous canonical conflict — when two sources of truth disagree
  • ✓ Legal / financial commitment
  • ✓ Irreversible / destructive action

The founder should not be

  • × Receipt courier — carrying receipts between agents
  • × Session memory — remembering state the system should hold
  • × PR scheduler — sequencing merges by hand
  • × Runtime watchdog — watching for failures the system should surface
  • × Handoff transport — moving work from one stage to the next
Why this matters: Every time the founder carries machine state, the graph has a hidden human edge. That edge does not scale, does not log, and does not survive absence.

Global stop conditions

Any one of these stops the line. They apply across all DAGs, not just the one where they were first noticed.

STOP · Identity conflict STOP · Unexpected dirty tree STOP · Different writer already active STOP · Missing authority STOP · Provider limit STOP · Semantic health failure STOP · Source / runtime mismatch STOP · Unproven public claim STOP · Scope expansion STOP · Receipt-chain break STOP · Duplicate-controller proposal

A stop is not a failure of the graph. It is the graph working — refusing to promote, publish, or proceed on unproven state.
