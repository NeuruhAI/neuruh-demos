ChatGPT said: Yeah, great question. The trick is starting ugly and fast. Don't wait for perfect.

Think of it like a rough draft storyboard. It's not art, it's just a map of the story.

Here's the simplest way people do it:

  1. Start with the Big Idea in one line Before you draw anything, write the core message in one sentence. Like: "Ordinary people can feel extraordinary when they wear this." That one line drives everything.

  2. Break it into beats, not shots Forget cameras. Just think in moments. What's the first feeling? What's the problem? What's the change? What's the payoff? Usually 5-7 beats is enough to start.

  3. Make a super rough 6-panel grid Grab a sheet of paper, divide it into 6 boxes. In each box: - Sketch a stick figure / super rough shape - Write 1-2 lines underneath: what happens + what is said/shown Don't spend more than 2 minutes per box.

Example for a launch: Scene 1: Person wakes up tired / ordinary Scene 2: Sees the problem (messy, slow, boring) Scene 3: Discovers the product Scene 4: Tries it - something clicks Scene 5: Life feels different / better Scene 6: Logo + tagline + call to action

  1. Add 3 notes under each panel - Visual: What do we see? - Audio: What do we hear / voiceover? - Feeling: What should the viewer feel?

That last one is the secret. If you nail the feeling, the visuals follow.

  1. Then refine in passes Pass 1: Ugly stick figures Pass 2: Add real copy / headlines Pass 3: Add timing, transitions, music cues Pass 4: Then give it to a designer / editor to polish

Want me to do it with you right now? Tell me what you're launching and I'll rough draft the 6 scenes for you in that format.

  14. Core Principle Taught Start ugly and fast. Don't wait for perfect. A rough draft storyboard is not art, it's a map of the story. Focus on feelings and beats, not cameras or shots.

  15. The Basic Rough Draft Process (General) Step 1: Start with the Big Idea in one line Example: "Ordinary people can feel extraordinary when they wear this." That one line drives everything.

Step 2: Break it into beats, not shots What's the first feeling? What's the problem? What's the change? What's the payoff? Usually 5-7 beats is enough to start.

Step 3: Make a super rough 6-panel grid Paper divided into 6 boxes. In each box: - Sketch a stick figure / super rough shape - Write 1-2 lines underneath: what happens + what is said/shown Don't spend more than 2 minutes per box.

Example launch arc: Scene 1: Person wakes up tired / ordinary Scene 2: Sees the problem (messy, slow, boring) Scene 3: Discovers the product Scene 4: Tries it - something clicks Scene 5: Life feels different / better Scene 6: Logo + tagline + call to action

Step 4: Add 3 notes under each panel - Visual: What do we see? - Audio: What do we hear / voiceover? - Feeling: What should the viewer feel? Feeling is the secret. If you nail the feeling, the visuals follow.

Step 5: Refine in passes Pass 1: Ugly stick figures Pass 2: Add real copy / headlines Pass 3: Add timing, transitions, music cues Pass 4: Give it to a designer / editor to polish
