01The Distinction

IQ and applied intelligence are not the same thing. The source draws the line plainly, and everything on this page follows from it.

The narrow question

IQ

How efficiently can this person solve standardized cognitive problems under controlled conditions?

Useful, but narrow. It measures performance on set problems, in set conditions, against a set standard.

The broader question

Applied Intelligence

How effectively does this person acquire information, detect structure, build models, reason under uncertainty, connect distant domains, and turn those models into functioning systems?

That is the question this report card is built to answer. It is broader, messier, and closer to what a founder, inventor, architect, or systems builder actually does.

03The GIA Composite

GIA stands for General Intelligence Architecture. It is a multidimensional cognitive-performance model, not a clinical IQ replacement.

GIA = Reasoning × Systems × Learning × Transfer × Synthesis × Execution × Judgment Multiplicative, not additive. A weakness in one factor pulls the whole composite down. That is the point.

Explicitly not a simple average

A person with exceptional reasoning but terrible epistemic discipline should not receive the same overall assessment as someone with equivalent reasoning who reliably distinguishes fact from hypothesis. An average would hide that difference. A multiplicative composite does not.

The formula above uses seven factors, which are the composite’s headline terms. The full profile in Section 2 has fifteen dimensions plus Judgment; the seven factors are the source’s own shorthand for the composite, not a claim that the other dimensions do not matter.
