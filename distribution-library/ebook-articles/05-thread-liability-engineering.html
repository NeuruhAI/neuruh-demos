1/10 Your CFO doesn't care how smart your robot is. He cares who pays when it crashes, and whether you can prove what happened. Capability gets you into the room. Insurability gets the signature.

2/10 An underwriter has one job: turn your operation into a price. They don't care about your model architecture. They care about frequency, severity, independence, and evidence. No evidence, no price.

3/10 Insurance math assumes failures arrive one at a time. Robot fleets share one model backbone across vendors. When the model has a bad day, it has it everywhere at once. Correlated risk needs bigger reserves, or it breaks the pool.

4/10 Carriers didn't exit AI risk because robots are uninsurable. They exited because the risk was unpriceable: no authorization records, no per-action scores, no append-only history. Decks about capability don't insure outcomes.

5/10 The engineering answer: separate the planner from the authorizer. The system that wants to move is never the system that permits the move. Motors stay locked until authorization lands. Build the split while you're small. Retrofit is a rewrite.

6/10 Score every action for risk before it happens and log the score. An underwriter can't price a fleet, but they can price a distribution of scored actions. Ten million scored actions is an actuarial object. Ten million unscored actions is a prayer.

7/10 Keep an append-only record from the pilot's first shift: proposal, score, authorization, outcome, in a form nobody can rewrite. When something breaks, the investigator reads the record instead of reconstructing it from finger-pointing.

8/10 Gate your updates. An over-the-air model push is the highest-correlation event in the system: one push changes the whole fleet at once. Staged rollouts, canary fleets, rollback rights in the contract. No silent pushes, ever.

9/10 Shadow-price your risk now. Compute the per-action price, accumulate the ledger, report it. You get real-time cost visibility and a dataset no one else has. When the carrier conversation happens, you arrive with evidence.

10/10 Start Monday: write liability allocation into the contract before deployment, confirm coverage in writing, register the fleet. Boring compliance is a sales accelerator. Details at neuruh.com. Questions: cal.com/neuruh
