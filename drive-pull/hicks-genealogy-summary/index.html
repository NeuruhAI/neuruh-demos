# 12. The Cherokee Hicks family: historically real, but not yet connected to the proven spine

This is where older genealogy documents got overenthusiastic. There absolutely is a historically important Cherokee Hicks family. What we do not currently have is the missing paper bridge proving that the William Hicks above descended from it.

The historically documented centerpiece is Charles Renatus Hicks, born 1767, one of the major Cherokee leaders of the early nineteenth century. Public historical documentation identifies him as a Cherokee leader, Second Principal Chief, and Principal Chief in 1827.

[Sources: Wikipedia]

A historical marker at Springplace likewise identifies Charles as born in 1767 at Tomatly, son of trader Nathan Hicks and a Cherokee mother.

The general Cherokee Hicks structure we have investigated is approximately:


William was Charles's brother and succeeded him temporarily in Cherokee leadership after Charles's death in 1827.

[Sources: Wikipedia]

# 13. Charles Renatus Hicks

Charles is the most important historical Hicks in the Cherokee branch.

The evidence we have around him includes:

◦ born December 23, 1767;

[Sources: Wikipedia]

[Sources: WCU]

So this is not vague Internet genealogy. Charles was sitting at the center of the creation of the modern Cherokee government: the constitutional, legal, educational, and diplomatic machinery of the Cherokee Nation.

# 24. Nathan Hicks and the possibility of a colonial bridge

Historically, Nathan Hicks: father of Charles and William in the Cherokee branch: was himself a European trader who entered Cherokee country from the colonial frontier. That means an important genetic point follows: Cherokee Hicks males descended paternally from Nathan would themselves carry a European Y chromosome, not a Native American Y chromosome. Therefore an I1/I-L22 result does not disprove a Cherokee Hicks connection.

[file preview: DNA_FRAMEWORK_2026-07-12.md]

This is why our best DNA experiment is not "is the Y chromosome Native American?" That is the wrong question. The correct question is: Does the direct Hicks Y chromosome match a documented direct male-line descendant of Nathan Hicks / the Cherokee Hicks family? That is the experiment capable of materially supporting or refuting the paternal connection.

[file preview: DNA_FRAMEWORK_2026-07-12.md]

# 36. What is actually remarkable here

Even after stripping out every romantic or unsupported ancestry claim, the surviving Hicks story is still remarkable. There is already documentary evidence for: an Ohio/Indiana Hicks family with a multigenerational printing tradition; a nineteenth-century William Hicks whose son Cyrus became a Columbus printer; and a separate, historically real Cherokee Hicks family whose leaders shaped the Cherokee Nation.

The unresolved question is not whether the Cherokee Hicks family existed. It plainly did. The unresolved question is whether these two Hicks systems converge.

```
OHIO PRINTING HICKS
William → Cyrus → Raymond → Raymond → Steven → Jeramie

                    ?

CHEROKEE HICKS
Nathan → Charles → William → Elijah → George ?
```

That question remains open. And now, unlike the original research, we know exactly where the proof must occur: at or immediately above William Hicks, c.1829-1831. The broader audit reached the same bottom-line ruling: Cyrus is substantially tied to William and Mary through the death certificate, while everything above William remains hypothesis, not pedigree.
