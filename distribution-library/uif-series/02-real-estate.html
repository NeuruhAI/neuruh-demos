Real Estate — The County Record Is the Circuit. Everything Else Is Noise.

For: tax-sale buyers and land investors, Indiana and Michigan Alt headline: Your AI Read the Parcel Card Wrong Keyword: AI parcel research

Tax-sale lists drop with hundreds of parcels and a short clock. So investors paste parcel cards and assessor pages into a chatbot and ask for the summary: acreage, zoning, liens, access. The summary comes back fluent. Sometimes it's right. Sometimes the acreage came from the wrong parcel ID, "no known liens" means the model never saw a lien search, and "road frontage" is a guess about what rural lots usually have. You bid on the summary. You own the parcel.

The field that broke: Any field the summary states as fact that the record doesn't contain. A missing document gets written up as a clean result.

The missing pin: The error signal. The model had no approved way to say "not in record," so it filled the gap with something plausible.

The fix: The model sees only the county documents for that one parcel ID. Nothing from memory, nothing from the parcel next door. Every stated field cites its document: assessor card, recorded deed, treasurer record. Missing fields print as NOT IN RECORD, in capitals, where you can't skim past them. Lien and title questions stay flagged for title work. The summary tells you what to verify; it doesn't verify it.

Fast research is worth paying for. Confident research built on a gap is how you end up owning a landlocked strip of swamp.

Where else this shows up: Every industry has its own "not in record." A grower's missing harvest log (Agriculture). A federal requirement nobody extracted (Defense). A payer policy section that doesn't exist (Healthcare Admin). The fix is the same each time: give the model an approved way to say the record is silent, and make that answer impossible to skim past.
