Freight — The Rate Con Is a Contract. Wire the Parser Like a Breaker Panel.

For: small carriers (5–50 trucks) and the brokers who book them Alt headline: The Detention Clause Your Parser Skipped Keyword: rate confirmation automation

Every load runs on a rate confirmation: linehaul, fuel, pickup and delivery windows, detention terms, lumper and TONU rules. A small back office handles dozens a week, so AI parsing into the TMS looks like free time. Then the parser grabs the linehaul from the previous load in the email thread. It drops the line that says detention pays after two hours. It reads the delivery window from the broker's footer template instead of the load details. The invoice goes out short, or it goes out wrong and sits in a dispute for 45 days. Cash flow in trucking is already tight. Leaking accessorials makes it tighter.

The fields that broke: Rate, accessorials, windows, and load numbers — every field that becomes money on the invoice.

The missing pins: The fuse and the error signal. Nothing checked parsed values against the signed rate con, and nothing flagged what was missing.

The fix: Each parsed field cites the line of the rate con it came from, and the load number must match on every page. A missing signature or a term that changed after signing blocks the load from invoicing until someone looks. Detention is computed by code from check-in and check-out timestamps against the signed terms, not estimated by the model. The invoice draft lists every accessorial owed, with its source line, so nothing gets left on the table quietly. The parser still saves the typing. It just can't sign you up for a rate you didn't agree to.

Where else this shows up: A signed document is a contract wherever it lives: a rate con, a subcontract, a lease, a purchase order. When a parser turns it into data, every field that becomes money or a date should cite the line it came from. If it can't, the field doesn't move downstream. The same discipline applies to quantities on an estimate (Insurance) and dates on a lien calendar (Construction).
