1/10 Your agents have two costs. The compute cost, which the vendor quotes you. And the attention cost, which nobody quotes you and which is usually larger. A thread on how to measure what your agents actually cost you.

2/10 Attention is not just minutes. It is your highest-value minutes. The only input that does judgment, relationships, capital allocation. Every minute spent re-prompting a drifting agent is a minute not spent on what only you can do.

3/10 Run a two-week attention audit. List every agent system and automation, including the ones you forgot about. For ten business days, log every minute you spend on each: reading, fixing, re-prompting, explaining, approving.

4/10 In the same window, count VERIFIED outcomes per system. Revenue collected, not pipeline. Leads contacted with logged responses, not touches sent. Tasks completed is volume. Verified outcomes are value.

5/10 Compute two numbers per system. Attention cost per outcome: your minutes divided by verified outcomes. Attention tax rate: your minutes divided by total system minutes. The second tells you how much of the "autonomous" system is actually you.

6/10 Now find which category dominates. Reading dominates: you are the verification layer. Fixing: you are the error handler. Re-prompting: you are its memory. Approving: the authority design is wrong. The fix follows the diagnosis.

7/10 Compute your manual baseline before judging anything: verified value per hour of doing the work yourself. If your agent stack cannot beat your manual density, it is not leverage. It is an expensive hobby.

8/10 Budget attention like money. "This system gets 90 minutes of my week." When it needs more, it does not get more of you. It gets a better design, or it waits. Constraints force the engineering.

9/10 Kill or fix anything below your manual baseline. A tool you have used for a year that scores below baseline is a habit, not an asset. This one decision beats any model upgrade.

10/10 The firewall rule: the machine interrupts you only when authority, genuine judgment, credentials, destructive recovery, or an unresolvable contradiction requires it. Everything else: solve it. Adapted from my book The Attention Economy of AI. More at neuruh.com
