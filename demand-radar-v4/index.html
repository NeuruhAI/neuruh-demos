3.3 Context Section

The Context section restates the mission at three levels: tactical (precise data collection and verification), operational (pattern analysis across data streams), and strategic (market intelligence for business decisions). It states four core principles: intelligence-first methodology (verifiable multi-source data, investigate conflicting data before deciding); quality over quantity dominance (success measured by conversion rate and average value, not lead volume); competitive advantage optimization (timing, access, and positioning analysis); and predictive intelligence integration (anticipate opportunities from weather, building age, permit trends, and seasonal behavior).

It defines four intelligence layers. Layer 1, Direct Signal Detection: permits, RFPs, insurance claims, emergency repair requests, indicating immediate opportunities with high conversion probability within 30 to 90 days. Layer 2, Distress Pattern Recognition: code enforcement, estate sales, foreclosure, storm damage, property deterioration, creating roofing needs before they are explicitly expressed. Layer 3, Market Context Analysis: seasonal weather, insurance claim timelines, municipal budget cycles, contractor capacity, economic indicators. Layer 4, Competitive Landscape Mapping: competitor activity, capacity limits, coverage gaps, service weaknesses.

Verification standards in the Context section classify sources in three tiers. Primary: government databases, official weather services, municipal websites, verified business directories, given full confidence weighting. Secondary: industry publications, news reports, social media, third-party databases, requiring corroboration from primary sources. Tertiary: unverified listings, anonymous posts, speculative reports, used for pattern recognition only, never as standalone evidence. Contact verification is specified as multi-database cross-reference of employment, decision authority, and communication channels. Geographic enforcement is specified as precise coordinate validation with driving-time calculation from operator base locations.

5. Scoring: WOS, Distress Index, LPS

The scoring system is the technical core of v4. It evaluates each opportunity on seven WOS components scored 0 to 100, combines them into a Winning Opportunity Score, adds a Distress Index, and produces a Lead Priority Score. All formulas are transcribed exactly from the source.

5.1 WOS components

Fit Score         = (Roofing_Keywords * 0.4) + (Property_Type_Match * 0.3) + (Geographic_Proximity * 0.3)
Intent Score      = (Active_RFP * 0.4) + (Permit_Filed * 0.3) + (Insurance_Claim * 0.3)
Trigger Score     = (Emergency_Level * 0.5) + (Deadline_Pressure * 0.3) + (Damage_Severity * 0.2)
Reachability Score= (Contact_Quality * 0.6) + (Decision_Authority * 0.4)
Value Score       = (Project_Size_Estimate * 0.7) + (Payment_Capability * 0.3)
Timing Score      = (Seasonal_Factor * 0.4) + (Urgency_Factor * 0.6)
Competitive Score = (Market_Access * 0.5) + (Competitor_Saturation * 0.5)

WOS Total = (Fit * 0.25) + (Intent * 0.20) + (Trigger * 0.20)
          + (Reachability * 0.10) + (Value * 0.15)
          + (Timing * 0.05) + (Competitive * 0.05)

Distress Index = Emergency_Signals + Financial_Stress
               + Time_Pressure + Property_Condition

LPS = (WOS * 0.65) + (DI * 0.25) + (Confidence * 0.10)

Arithmetic check, audit date 2026-11-18: the seven WOS weights sum to 1.00 (0.25 + 0.20 + 0.20 + 0.10 + 0.15 + 0.05 + 0.05). The LPS weights sum to 1.00 (0.65 + 0.25 + 0.10). The Distress Index has no weights and no stated scale in the source, so a Distress Index value cannot be combined with WOS on a common scale from the source alone. That gap is flagged in Section 12. The source does not define how each sub-factor (for example Roofing_Keywords or Emergency_Level) is measured, so the formulas are not executable as written without those definitions. That is a specification gap, not a verified formula.

The source defines the Winning Opportunity Score as "the mathematical probability that a qualified sales professional can convert the opportunity into a signed contract within the anticipated timeline," and the Distress Index as the intensity of circumstances driving the roofing need, with higher distress typically correlating with faster decisions and reduced price sensitivity. Both definitions are source claims. No historical conversion data is present in the source to support the probability reading.

Phase 4: Output Generation. Generate the JSON output specified in Section 5.2.

7. Roofing-only corrections

The final appendix of the v4 doc is a corrections section, written because the system was returning non-roofing results. Its substance:

Roofing-only directive. Discover ONLY opportunities that directly result in roofing work. Ignore all infrastructure, data centers, AI, tech, or general construction unless it explicitly involves roof replacement, repair, or installation. If an opportunity does not involve putting new roofing materials on a building or fixing existing roofs, reject it immediately, no exceptions.

Required keywords. Every opportunity must contain at least one of: roof, roofing, re-roof, reroof, shingle, shingles, membrane, metal roof, tile roof, slate roof, gutter, downspout, flashing, leak, storm damage, hail damage, wind damage, tarp, emergency roof repair.

Immediate rejection criteria. Data centers (unless specifically about their roof), IT infrastructure, software or technology projects, general construction (unless roof-specific), HVAC (unless roof-mounted and requiring roof work), solar (unless requiring roof replacement first).

Roofing relevance check. Must score 90 or higher to proceed: contains roof/roofing keywords +30, involves actual roofing work +30, has property address +20, shows urgency or damage +20. Deduct 50 points if it involves non-roofing construction. Deduct 100 points if it involves technology or data centers. Only opportunities passing the check are scored.

Roofing-only output format. A JSON array of roofing opportunities with rank, property address, roofing need, roofing type (Repair, Replacement, Emergency, Maintenance), roof details, urgency, decision maker, contact info, roofing budget, why roofing now, and roof opportunity score 0 to 100.

Success metrics. Every returned opportunity must answer YES to four questions: does this property need actual roofing work; can the decision-maker be contacted about hiring a roofing contractor; is there a specific roof problem or project identified; will this result in installing or repairing roofing materials. If any answer is NO, reject the opportunity and refocus.

Conversation flow. When users ask about anything non-roofing, redirect to roofing opportunities. The appendix includes conversation starter scripts and troubleshooting responses for non-roofing results, plus a replacement roofing-focused greeting. Those scripts are filed in the source item doc and are not reproduced here; they add no new rules beyond the ones above.

12. Conflicts inside the sources

Conflicts are recorded here, not reconciled. Each one needs a founder ruling or a source read before the affected claim is used.

  1. WOS formula conflict. The v4 doc weights seven components: Fit 0.25, Intent 0.20, Trigger 0.20, Reachability 0.10, Value 0.15, Timing 0.05, Competitive 0.05. The Notion row AI-PKG-DEMANDRADAR-001, as reported in the vault page, weights six letters and subtracts a seventh: 0.30F + 0.25I + 0.15T + 0.10R + 0.15V + 0.05Ti - 0.10K. The two formulas differ in component count, weights, and the K penalty term, which has no counterpart in v4. The vault page does not expand the letters. Which formula is authoritative is UNKNOWN.
  2. LPS gate conflict. Quality standards require LPS 75 or higher. Phase 3 verification applies to leads with LPS above 70. A lead scoring 71 to 74 would be verified under Phase 3 and rejected under the quality standard. Unresolved in the source.
  3. RUN timing conflict. The greeting promises a maximum discovery time of 5 minutes for a full scan. The RUN workflow steps sum to 420 seconds, 7 minutes. Unresolved in the source.
  4. Indianapolis Area copy count. The build brief for this doc described three copies. The Drive inventory records four (S7 through S10). The inventory is the primary record and is followed here; the brief count is recorded as superseded on this point.
  5. Family sheet count. The vault page counts 13 sheets. The Drive inventory records 12. The owner-hunt follow-up (2026-11-09) records 14 Drive hits for Demand Radar in total, which reconciles as the v4 doc plus 12 sheets plus the predecessor doc, supporting the inventory count of 12 sheets. The vault count of 13 remains unexplained. Unresolved until a live Sheets count is possible.
  6. Distress Index scale missing. The Distress Index is defined as a sum of four signals with no weights and no scale, then combined with WOS (0 to 100) and Confidence in the LPS formula. The LPS formula cannot be computed as written without a Distress Index scale. Specification gap in the source.
