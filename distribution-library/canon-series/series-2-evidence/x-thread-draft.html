Series 2 — Evidence Precedes Confidence: X thread draft (@NeuruhAI)

Thread: 9 posts. Plain text, no link except final post if approved. All numbers carry the June 30, 2026 audit date.

1/ Most founders are storytellers first. On June 30 I ran an adversarial diligence check on my own estate and killed my own numbers. Everything I used to say about valuations is void. Here's what survived.

2/ The kill list. $6M indicative valuation. $750K to $1.75M. $15-25M pre-money. 801 IP entries. 858 assets. 130 repos sold as 130 products. "Quantum-proof." "Palantir-class." None of it survived contact with running code. All of it: SUPERSEDED. Kept for history, never cited again.

3/ What survived got a Tier A stamp. Tier A means code-verified: running code, passing tests, a deployed artifact, or a commit I can point at. Tier B means built and inspectable, but not independently verified. Tier C means a document exists. Documents don't execute.

4/ Tier A numbers, June 30 audit: 43 working codebases. 4 live. 1 deployed product engine. About 8 revenue-sellable product lines. 148 tests green on the scoring model. 49 signals. 43,897 records scored. 31 named IP frameworks.

5/ And MRR: $0. That's Tier A too. I put the zero in the same tier as the code, because honesty about the zero is what makes the code numbers believable.

6/ The rule now: no dollar valuation is claimable pre-MRR. Not indicative, not conservative, not back-of-napkin. The valuation conversation waits for revenue.

7/ Why do this publicly? An investor's diligence runs the exact check I ran. Publishing only what survives it changes what you are to them: from a narrative risk into a verifiable counterparty.

8/ The hard part wasn't the audit. It was deleting the sentences that used to make me sound impressive. Every founder has a set. Go find yours.

9/ Next thread: how I keep the AI from shipping itself to production. The factory state machine. Receipts first. - NeurOt
