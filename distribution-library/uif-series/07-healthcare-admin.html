Healthcare Admin — Denial Appeals Are a Grounding Problem, Not a Writing Problem

For: independent dental and physical therapy practices Alt headline: The Policy Section That Doesn't Exist Keyword: AI claim denial appeal letters

Denials pile up, appeals have filing windows, and nobody went to dental school to write letters to payers. AI looks like the obvious fix: paste the denial, get a persuasive appeal. The letter is persuasive. It also cites a procedure code you didn't bill, quotes a payer policy section that doesn't exist, and describes findings that aren't in the chart for that date of service. A reviewer checks the citation, finds nothing, and the appeal is done — along with some of your credibility on the next one.

The fields that broke: Codes, dates of service, clinical findings, and policy citations. A good appeal is mostly evidence; a polished letter with bad evidence is worse than a plain one with good evidence.

The missing pins: Input context and the fuse. The model wrote from general knowledge about how appeals usually read, not from this patient's chart and this payer's policy.

The fix: The model sees three things only: the chart notes for that date of service, the denial and EOB, and the payer's published policy document. Every code, date, and finding in the letter must match the chart. Every policy quote must match the payer document, with its section number. Anything that doesn't trace is blocked and listed for the biller, not smoothed over. The clinician or office manager signs off before anything is sent.

Patient data comes first. Before any chart touches an AI system, confirm where the data runs and that every vendor handling it is covered by a business associate agreement. If that can't be satisfied, the build stops there. Your compliance lead makes the final call.

Where else this shows up: Polished writing on top of unchecked evidence fails everywhere. Proposals answer requirements the solicitation never stated (Defense). Listings describe marks nobody photographed (Resale). The writing is the cheap part. Lock the evidence first, then let the model write around it.
