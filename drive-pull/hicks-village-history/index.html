# But there's an even deeper layer

Nevis was a major English/British sugar plantation island.

The area around Hick's Village wasn't some isolated little Caribbean settlement that appeared recently. It was part of a dense plantation landscape, with neighbouring estates including New River Estate, Eden Brown Estate and Coconut Walk Estate, among others.

Sources: Wikipedia, 2 sources

The historical plantation system also means we have to separate the Hicks surname/property from the much larger population that lived and worked there.

This is particularly important for genealogy.

### Hicks Estate appears in historical plantation records

A compilation of Nevis plantation records from 1817 lists Hicks among the estates in Saint James Windward.

Sources: NevisNow

And by the 1830s, Hicks was one of the Saint James Windward estates with its own educational provision. A historical study records that in 1833, the Hicks estate school had 32 pupils. Those were children associated with the enslaved population of the estate system.

Sources: UoB Interactive Server

That gives us a very important historical context:

Hicks Estate existed within the plantation/slavery economy of Nevis before emancipation.

Britain abolished slavery in its Caribbean colonies in 1834, with the transition to full freedom completed after the apprenticeship period in 1838. The historical work on Nevis documents that Hicks Estate existed in that plantation economy.

Sources: Paperzz, 2 sources
