Site Selection

Business counterpart: Market plus Strategy Fit

What winners do: Audit macro forces, customer behavior, regulatory climate before you spend a dollar. Plant in fertile ground, then stay there long enough to exploit compounding advantages.

What wannabes do: Chase shiny TAM slides, ignore regulatory sinkholes, re-plant every six months.

Five non-negotiables before you dig the hole

  1. Geotechnical Survey. Validate unmet demand, purchasing power, and regulatory landmines. A maple seed in the Mojave does not "fail fast"; it just dies.
  2. Rootstock Selection. Pick a corporate form and capitalization stack that can bear the ultimate height you intend (Delaware C-Corp, dual-class, tokenized rails: choose, document, commit).
  3. Soil Amendment. Preload capital buffers, founder alignment docs, and data pipelines. Cheap soil = stunted tree.
  4. Wind-break Installation. Pre-wire legal counsel, cyber insurance, crisis PR. You do not buy an umbrella after the hurricane.
  5. Planting Angulation. Set the trunk's initial direction: one flagship problem, one ICP, one GTM motion. The straighter the sapling, the taller the eventual crown.

Operating principles to keep growth vertical

  • Photosynthesize Daily: revenue is oxygen. No oxygen, no metabolism, no mission.
  • Prune Quarterly: kill pet projects that do not widen the canopy or deepen the roots. Sentimentality is rot.
  • Fertilize Intelligently: mentors, board members, and targeted capital are slow-release nutrients, apply sparingly, measure growth response.
  • Monitor Sap Flow: weekly cash dashboards; monthly cohort analyses. If flow slows, search for girdling insects (margin erosion, CAC creep).
  • Respect Dormancy: use macro downturns to refactor code, renegotiate vendor contracts, and deepen roots. Trees do not photosynthesize in January; they build capacity.
"Branching out before your trunk can handle it is like bolting a helicopter rotor to a toothpick. Impressive for the first rotation, catastrophic by the second."

Self-check: map one initiative

The doc's rule: every initiative must map to roots, trunk, or canopy. Until your trunk product funds payroll and R and D, any new "branch" is a parasite.

Takeaway playbook

  • Blueprint First: codify the tree model in your strategy doc; every initiative must map to roots, trunk, or canopy.
  • Capital Hygiene: treat cash-flow models and compliance audits as root feeders, not paperwork.
  • Relentless Focus: until your trunk product funds payroll and R and D, any new "branch" is a parasite.
  • Environmental Stewardship: cultivate adjacent talent pools, partner ecosystems, and policy goodwill; they are the rain cycle your tree depends on.
  • Iterate, Don't Transplant: solve defects in place; uprooting a half-grown tree bleeds compounding advantage.
