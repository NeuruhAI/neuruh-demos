1. The stands and the arena

Life has a horrible way of reminding us of a few different things. One of them is this: the person having the experience and the person watching it are not in the same place.

From the stands it is very easy to say the cliche thing, the obvious thing. You are looking at something, so time is spent there, so nothing else is getting done. That is the obvious answer. Then you make an observation, give an opinion, put it out into the world, and repeat the pattern.

What gets missed is what it is like wearing those shoes. Sitting in the stands, it is easy to perceive what it takes. You are not out there on the court. You are not out there in the arena. You are not actually out there earning your stripes. And so the perception of what is, generally, is not going to be true facts.

There is a huge psychological component to this. People in psychology say behavior is predictable. On patterns and signals, that is partly disagreed with here. If a behavior is being shown or done, the question is why.

2. Attention has weight

Look at the reality projected through your eyes and what you decide to give your attention to. It could be online, where certain places can target you with messages. It could be real life. And there are the things upstream of all of it that led to all of it.

You have an input you are allowing to come in. Then you make a judgment or an action based on your output. But you cannot conceive or take in all the variables of the outside.

From a weighted metric, the effect is concrete. Online, reading something, a certain phrase or word pattern is going to weigh way heavier than something else. Think of that at scale, doing it and receiving it over time, and you end up able to draw a picture that is kind of clear on your ability and where you lack.

That is the system at work: constantly changing inputs to hopefully align with outputs, for anyone who wants to improve.

3. The observer problem

When you are trying to find center and balance, and someone from an outside perspective gives an opinion, and you are 20 or 30 years into doing something, there is going to be a huge disconnect. The disconnect is not necessarily that either side is right or wrong. The disconnect is that there is not enough context to make a decision or an observation.

We lean, especially from a philosophical side, toward wanting to be the observer, as opposed to observing ourselves and what that looks like.

The correction is to weigh an observation for exactly what it is: a small glimpse in a moment in time. It does not set the standard for anything. Look at the average over time, in linear space, from a bird's eye view, to judge and understand what is actually going on. That gives a better assessment of the entire situation than a small time frame that matches your cognitive bias.

You look at someone and see them looking at stuff, and of course he is wasting time. You have no idea what he is potentially doing. These are judgments, and they have to be handled as judgments.

4. Ego death and the false center

When the conversation drifts into the metaphysical, as above so below, within without, the only way to really have that experience or that observation is by ego death. And the only way to have an ego death is going through experience.

This is spoken from experience of quite a few ego deaths. It is not what people perceive.

When you get into the realm of higher consciousness and try to find a center in something, what you ultimately end up getting to is, in this view, a false reality. You are LARPing your way into a state of being that is centered or whole, to be present. The consensus says do not lean too far into your sword, do not lean too far into your gardener side. But life does not work that way. Life is very dynamic. It is not static. It is not absolute.

A dynamic environment, with dynamic memories and the ability to discern information within a live atmosphere, does not hold still for a fixed center.

5. The recursive loop

In his case: I cannot do anything right, so I am constantly stuck in a recursive loop that I am trying to fix. As I try to grow and see the stuff inside, I become hyper aware of it. That does not necessarily mean understanding it from the beginning, but the hyper awareness is generally there.

As you start discerning a lot of the information, it becomes complicated. The last few months have been very complicated. As things have started to converge, the complication becomes a little less. That does not mean the same patterns and signals are gone. It may mean you are not paying as much attention, or not doing something enough, or not conflating your data pool with a whole bunch of outside sources.

When your discernment is zeroing in on something, you are going to miss stuff on the outside. The pursuit of anything works this way. Seeking love is the example here, and it is the best example of the pattern in general.

On the analytical side, the pain points and weaknesses are known. A lot of what gets said about them is going to be an excuse at the end of the day. There is really no other way around it. You cannot bat a thousand. You bat 995. It comes down to the habits and the patterns. If you are allowing some of these things to happen, why?

6. After thirteen years

For instance, coming out of a situation with thirteen years of drama, weird stuff, stolen things, cheating, infidelity, and not receiving love as you perceived it: that ends up ranking way higher than anything else. Because you finally have an opportunity where the clarity held long enough for something new to emerge. That new emergence is finally being able to seek love.

When you get into a pattern in a new situation that has emerged in an environment, and an outsider is looking in from a shell that is months old or has no context, it is never going to be the correct window. It does not matter how you try to frame it to fill it in. It ends up being LARP, essentially.

Opinions and insight on this are a double-edged sword. If the observer is seeing and starts weighing in, and in this case the person cannot do anything right, the loop tightens.

On the personal accounting: so much work done, still so far behind. A completely broken soul, in his own words. On the analytical side, the pain points and weaknesses are known. Both can be true at once.

7. Learn, don't be right

Coming into a space with AI and SI, the hardest truth for a lot of people is that they are not willing to accept some of these truths, because they want to be right. We should not strive to be right. We should essentially strive to learn.

That really comes down to being able to question ourselves. Who do we really know who we are? How can you define that you really know who you are? Have you even tested who you are before?

It is very easy to say you went four days not eating, or you have a whole bunch of money in your bank. That is the easy version. Can you really do it with nothing, or in a very hard thing where you have to carry weight and a whole bunch of things? The answer, honestly, is no. I cannot even do some of that stuff. That is why we grow and we accept who we are.

8. Polymaths and the information fabric

At some point you come across someone who can do way more than you can, like a polymath. You question how they are such a polymath. It is the example of all of this: the discernment across all the different areas that you are able to converge onto something, so that new system is a new system.

The observer who is still running on an old system, or one or two things, or claiming consciousness, is irrelevant. Because at the end of the day it is all order and structure in a systems world, in a data world, where at a very nuanced level a bit, a node, is a piece of information. We live in an information fabric, taking in stuff through ears and eyes and observations, trying to converge to a conclusion.

That conclusion is not necessarily going to align to true north. It does not mean you are even wrong. It just does not mean you are 100 percent right either.

9. Protect your peace

Me personally, I do not give a fuck. I would rather protect my peace and who I am than have to deal with any LARP, unless you are willing to help, able to stand on business, able to say, hey, this very specific thing. Outside of that, based on my environment and everything I have, I do not care who you are. It could come across as disrespectful, but you have not earned that right. You have not earned that right for me.

It is the same for anyone. We have to be respectful and mindful of people's situations. If you want things to emerge, and you want polarity to finally pop on both ends, it is going to have to come down to two people being wrong, in a sense. That is what allows communication to happen. If you are both too right on something, you just keep arguing and pulling. It is like two trucks pulling against each other. It is not until one truck kind of gives up that you can pull backwards. So we have to understand give and take.

For me, it is so much easier to stay away from trying to judge someone. I have met thousands and thousands of people, actually had conversations, been in houses, touched millions and millions of items. That gives a different data set to pull from and make decisions with. I can tell you with high confidence that a lot of assumptions about people are just completely wrong. You are generally missing context or information.
