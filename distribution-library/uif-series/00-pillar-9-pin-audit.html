Pillar — The 9-Pin Audit: Inspect Your AI Agent Like a Circuit Board

Alt headline: Your AI Mostly Works. That's the Problem. Keyword: AI agent audit

Most AI deployments that "mostly work" fail the same way. Nobody can say what's wired to what. An electrician doesn't trust a panel because it hasn't caught fire yet. They open it, trace every circuit, and check that the breakers match the load. AI systems deserve the same inspection, and almost none get it.

The model is a chip. Everything else is the board. Neuruh's Universal Interfacing Framework treats a language model as a processor: powerful, fast, and not fully predictable. The reliability comes from what you wire around it. So you audit the wiring, nine pins at a time.

Pin Signal What to check
1 Power Which model and provider, who pays, what happens when it's down
2 Trigger What event starts a run, and whether anyone knows
3 Standing instructions What it's told every time, and who last changed it
4 Randomness Whether outputs that should be fixed are allowed to vary
5 Input context Exactly which documents it can see
6 Error signal Whether it can say "not in the source" instead of guessing
7 Tool access What systems it can write to, and with what limits
8 Interrupt What stops it mid-run
9 Output Where its words and numbers land, and who reads them first

Three components most builds are missing:

The fuse. It sits on the output line. Any field that drives a decision — a dollar amount, a date, a code, a measurement, a brand name — has to trace back to a source document, or it doesn't pass. This is a trace check, not a guess about whether the model "seems confident."

The router. It sends each job to the cheapest component that can carry it: routine work to automation, ambiguous work to a stronger model, high-stakes work to a person.

The override. A person can stop or reverse any action, and the system records that they did.

Why circuit language? The people accountable for these systems — operations managers, adjusters, estimators, compliance leads — already think in breakers, interlocks, and lockout. The vocabulary lets them audit an AI system without learning machine learning first.

Run it on your own system. Pick one AI workflow you already run. Answer each pin in a sentence. Any pin where the honest answer is "not sure" is floating, and floating pins are where incidents start. Then list every field the system outputs that someone acts on: a price, a date, a code, a name. Next to each, write the document it traces to. The blanks are where the fuse goes.

One board, eleven industries. The rest of this series carries the same board into roofing estimates, parcel research, rent rolls, lien deadlines, machine-shop quotes, after-hours receptionists, payer appeals, rate confirmations, federal proposals, harvest logs, and estate listings. The paperwork changes every time. The failures don't.
