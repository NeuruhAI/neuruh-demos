Series 2 — Evidence Precedes Confidence: LinkedIn article draft

Title: I Ran Diligence on Myself and Voided My Own Valuations

Subtitle: What survived, what didn't, and the tier system that now governs every claim I make.

Most founders treat fundraising like storytelling. I did too. I had numbers in my mouth: $6M indicative, $750K to $1.75M, $15-25M pre-money. 801 IP entries. 858 assets. 130 repos framed as 130 products. Language like "quantum-proof" and "Palantir-class."

On June 30, 2026, I ran an adversarial diligence check against my own estate. I had an auditor's mindset and full access. The result: none of the legacy figures survived contact with running code. All of them are now stamped SUPERSEDED. They are retained for historical record only. I will never cite them as current again.

That audit produced something better than a valuation: an evidence tier system that now governs every claim I make.

Tier A is code-verified. The claim is backed by running code, a passing test suite, a deployed artifact, or a commit I can anchor to. These numbers are claimable: in a data room, on a pitch deck, on a public post.

Tier B is built and inspectable. It exists. I can demonstrate it. But it hasn't been independently verified, so it can be described, never claimed as proof.

Tier C is specced, scoped, or aspirational. A design or scope document only. It does not execute. It is never surfaced externally.

The Tier A inventory as of June 30: 43 working codebases. 4 live. 1 deployed product engine. Roughly 8 revenue-sellable product lines. 148 tests passing on the land scoring model. 49 signals in the model. 43,897 records scored. 31 named IP frameworks.

And the one that matters most: $0 MRR. That is also Tier A. I put the zero in the same tier as the code because honesty about the zero is exactly what makes the code numbers believable.

The operating rule that came out of this: no dollar valuation is current or claimable before MRR exists. Not indicative, not conservative, not back-of-napkin. The valuation conversation waits for revenue.

There is a commercial logic to this discipline, not just an ethical one. An investor performing diligence will run precisely the check I ran on myself. Publishing only what survives it changes what you are to them: from a narrative risk into a verifiable counterparty. The founder who shows you the zero alongside the 148 tests is cheaper to diligence than the founder who hands you a $6M slide.

The hard part of the audit wasn't technical. It was deleting the sentences that used to make me sound impressive. Every founder has a set. The discipline is going to find yours before someone else does.

This is the second in a series on the internal canon I've operated on for two years and never shared. Next: the autonomous factory, and how I keep the AI from shipping itself to production.
