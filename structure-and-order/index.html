Rethinking Structure and Order

An argument about what things are, what they can do, and how new possibilities are made.

Structure determines possibility. Design is the selection of a possibility space. Change the structure and you change what is possible.

The Neuruh organism is this philosophy applied: a system of bounded structures with designed relationships, built to produce outcomes through architecture rather than exhortation.

Opening · The original argument, in Jeramie’s words

Everything is structure and order

“Everything is structure and order. We as humans don’t give constraints nor divide up the chores or duties needed to complete an objective. We do them through architecture, through design of structures, and through design of relationships. Every single thing we ever made is the exact same.”

The human body is a structure. Its body parts are a structure. Cells are a structure. Their subparts are a structure. Structure all the way down, and structures build upon structure.

Body parts are designed to act based on information they receive from the brain: designed to receive information, designed to interpret it, designed to act upon it. Organs are designed as a specific structure to accomplish a specific objective. If he wants his body to do a different thing, his muscles perform different motions, by changing structure or order.

To do something new that has never been done before, design a new structure or change the order or structure of existing structures. Change the structure and a new possibility opens.

The body takes in information from the environment, senses what is around it, decides what to do. What it does depends on the structure and on the information it receives. Information is data and it is also architecture: a physical arrangement of the world. Information is structure and order. The physical arrangement of molecules tells us something, like mass or charge.

All human creations are structures organized through design. A building is designed as a structure so we can live in it. A factory is designed as a structure so it can take in a raw material, process it, and produce a new product. Machines inside are structures for specific tasks: a conveyor belt moves materials, a drill cuts holes. A factory is itself a structure managing a collection of other structures. A company is designed the same way, built from individuals, departments, teams.

It is not merely structure. The relationships between structures matter and determine what structures are able to do. The body has internal structures (cells, tissues, organs, organ systems) and relationships with outside structures (environment, other humans, tools).

Information comes from structure. Structure dictates the information it produces. That information informs other structures, which react and produce their own information: a never-ending cycle.

Human knowledge is information and structure: the relationship between facts, ideas, principles, learned from interacting with structures and the environment. Human language is a structure of words and grammar, rules and conventions. Its structure determines what it can express. The meaning of a sentence is not in the words themselves but in the structure of the language.

To achieve something, define the structure and the design of the relationships between those structures. All of this is design. All of this is architecture.

The catalog

Designing a building, business, machine, computer, company, factory, organization, language, computer program, website, city, government, school, curriculum, product, service, process, system: each is designing a structure. Designing a new structure or changing relationships creates a new possibility, a new way of doing things, being, thinking, living.

The structure of a thing determines what it is able to do, its behavior, its function, its purpose, its meaning, its value, its identity, its existence.

Section I

The thesis, stated with maximum precision

Everything that exists, persists, acts, or means anything does so because of structure and order. This is not metaphor. It is physics, logic, constraint, possibility.

A thing is its structure: its arrangement, its relationships, its position in a larger structure. Change the arrangement, the relationships, the scale, the information flow, or the constraints, and you change the thing.

The negative form

No behavior without structure. No function without arrangement. No meaning without relationship. No action without a system that makes it possible, probable, easy, difficult, or impossible.

Section II · Figure 1

Fundamental ontology: structure all the way down and up

At each level, the lower constrains the higher, the higher constrains the lower, the higher is not reducible to the lower. That irreducibility is emergence. The lower sets physical limits; the higher sets the pattern the lower must serve.

Water is not wet because hydrogen is wet. Wetness is structural. Consciousness is not in a neuron. It emerges from 86 billion neurons, connections, firing patterns, feedback loops, integration. A company is not in a single employee.

Section III · Figure 2

A formal definition of structure

A structure is defined by five elements. Together they are the complete anatomy of anything that works, persists, or means. Change any one of them and the system changes.

Section IV

Humans do not directly cause outcomes; humans design architectures that cause outcomes

The naive model: I want X, I do X, X happens. The real model: I want X, I design a structure S in which X is the likely, stable, repeatable outcome, and S produces X.

SystemThe structureWhat emerges
FactoryLayout, machine sequence, supply chain, division of labor, quality control, incentives, management hierarchyCars emerge. Remove the structure and no cars are produced, no matter how much anyone wants them.
BodyNervous system, musculoskeletal system, metabolic system, sensory system, brainMovement emerges.
CompanyRoles, reporting lines, decision rights, communication channels, tools and data access, compensation and promotion, culturePerformance emerges.
ComputerTransistors to logic gates to circuits to processor to memory and I/O to operating system to applicationsComputation emerges.

Design is indirect control. Architecture is the lever. Structure is the mechanism. If you want a different outcome, do not exhort harder. Redesign the structure.

Section V · Figure 3

Information is structure that can be read

Information is a physical difference that makes a causal difference to a system capable of detecting it.

The formalization

Information = a pattern in a physical arrangement + a reader structured to detect that pattern and respond to it. Without the reader, only arrangement. Without the arrangement, only a reader waiting.

Examples: a DNA sequence read by ribosomes, polymerases, regulatory networks. A molecule’s shape, charge, and mass as information when another molecule’s binding site fits or does not. Neural firing patterns integrated by downstream neurons. Language parsed by a brain with grammar, lexicon, shared conventions. A price in a database responded to by buyers, sellers, algorithms.

The identity

Architecture is structure arranged to act. Information is structure arranged to be read. Knowledge is structure arranged to predict. Language is structure arranged to communicate. Money is structure arranged to coordinate. Law is structure arranged to constrain.

Section VI

Relationships are ontologically primary

Parts are not primary. Relationships determine what parts are, what they can do, what they mean. A part in isolation has potential. In relation, it has function.

Three proofs

A heart alone is a mass of muscle. It contracts and does nothing useful. A heart in a system, with lungs, arteries, nerves, hormones, a body, has a function, and that function is in the network.

A word alone is a sound, a shape. A word in a sentence, as subject, object, verb, modifier, takes meaning from position, grammar, context, conventions.

A person alone has limited strength, knowledge, reach. A person in a network, with tools, people, institutions, knowledge, can do what no isolated person can.

Formal principle

Behavior is a property of the system, not of the components. Change the components but keep the relationships, and the system often retains its behavior. Change the relationships but keep the components, and it becomes a different system.

A company can replace every employee over ten years and remain the same company if structure, processes, and culture persist. It can keep every employee and become a different company overnight if structure, incentives, and reporting lines change.

Structure is destiny.

Section VII

Design is the selection of a possibility space

A possibility space is all actions possible, probable, easy, hard, or impossible given a structure.

StructureEasyHard or impossible
A chairSittingStanding is possible but unstable; flying is impossible; sleeping is difficult
A factoryMass production, consistencyCustomization is hard; rapid change is expensive
A languageCertain thoughts, salient distinctionsOther thoughts are difficult; some are invisible without inventing new structure
A lawCertain behaviors, legal and normalOthers are illegal; others are unthinkable because unrepresented
A software systemCertain operations, fast; some data, accessibleOther operations are slow; other data is inaccessible

Good design makes the right things easy and the wrong things hard. Bad design, usually by accident, does the opposite: it makes the wrong thing easy and the right thing hard, creates perverse incentives, bottlenecks, single points of failure, hidden fragility.

Design is therefore ethical, political, cognitive, not neutral. To design a structure is to decide what kind of world you want to live in.

Section VIII · Figure 4

The two kinds of design

Intentional design and emergent design obey the same law: structure determines possibility. What differs is how you intervene.

Section IX

The correction about the body: evolution as a design process without a designer

The human body was not designed by a human. It was designed by evolution: variation (random mutation, recombination), selection (differential survival and reproduction), retention (heritable traits persist), iteration over billions of years.

Evolution is blind. It has no goal, no foresight, no model of the future. It optimizes for reproductive success in a past environment, not health, happiness, or longevity now.

It is brilliant and flawed. The eye, the hand, the brain, the immune system are masterpieces. The blind spot, back pain from bipedalism, the appendix, aging are artifacts of historical constraints and trade-offs.

The body is a palimpsest: layers built on older structures, repurposed, constrained by history. That is true of all emergent systems: historical baggage, not optimal, satisficing, good enough to survive. Intentional design can improve on them only if it understands their constraints.

Section X

Knowledge is structure. Language is structure. Thought is structure.

Knowledge

Knowledge is not a pile of facts but a structured network of facts, models, principles, methods: nodes, edges, models, methods. Learning is restructuring your knowledge graph. Expertise is the right structure: dense connections, correct causal links, efficient retrieval, accurate models. Ignorance is not lack of facts but a malformed knowledge structure.

Language

Language is not a bag of words but a structured system for encoding thought, at levels of phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics. Its structure determines what can be said easily, with difficulty, or not without inventing new structure. Meaning is in the structural relationship between words, grammar, context, shared assumptions. Change the structure, change the meaning. Translation is hard, poetry is powerful, legal language is precise and fragile.

Thought

Thought is the manipulation of structured representations: models, categories, hierarchies, analogies, narratives. To think better is to improve the structure of your models. To think differently is to adopt a different structure. Cognitive biases are structural flaws. Logical fallacies are structural errors. Creativity is restructuring a problem.

Section XI

Organizations, cities, governments, economies are structures made of structures

Company: individuals, teams, departments, divisions; reporting lines, communication channels, processes, incentives, law, market, resources. Change the org chart, the incentives, the tools, the culture, and you change the company. A company is a machine converting inputs into outputs, and the machine is its structure.

Factory: machines, layout, conveyors, pipes, wires, process sequence, control system, workers, safety rules, quality standards, production targets. The factory is not the building. It is the system operating inside it.

City: buildings, zones, roads, transit, utilities, laws, markets, norms, people with diverse goals, emergent traffic, commerce, culture, crime, innovation. A hybrid of intentional and emergent design.

Government: institutions, branches, checks and balances, a constitution as meta-structure, officials, law, elections, public opinion. Constitutions are architectures for power.

Economy: agents, markets, property rights, contract law, money; emergent prices, growth, inequality, innovation. Change the rules and you change what is produced, who benefits, what is incentivized.

Section XII

The meta-principle: structure determines everything downstream

Structure determines behavior, function, purpose, meaning, value, identity, possibility, existence. These are not separate properties but different descriptions of the same structural fact.

PropertyWhat it is, structurally
BehaviorStructure in action
FunctionStructure interpreted in terms of purpose
MeaningStructure interpreted by a reader
ValueStructure evaluated against a goal
IdentityStructure distinguished from other structures
ExistenceStructure maintaining itself against entropy

Change the structure and all of them change together. Behavior can be changed temporarily by force, willpower, or exhortation, but without structural change the system reverts to its structural equilibrium. This is why diets, reforms, and resolutions fail. Lasting change requires structural change.

Section XIII

The engineering implication: if you want a new outcome, redesign the architecture

Reframe the questions:

Instead of askingAsk
How do I get people to do X?What structure would make X the natural, easy, inevitable outcome?
How do I become more productive?What structure of my day, tools, environment, commitments would make productivity the default?
How do I make my company more innovative?What structure of incentives, information flow, psychological safety, resource allocation would make innovation the normal output?
How do I learn faster?What structure of knowledge, practice, feedback, spaced repetition would make learning efficient?
How do I build a better AI?What architecture of data, models, training, evaluation, deployment would make the desired behavior emerge reliably?

The test is empirical: does the new structure produce a better outcome, reliably, without violating constraints that actually matter, without creating new problems worse than the old ones? If yes, you engineered a better possibility. If no, you merely rearranged the furniture.

Section XIV

The limits of the thesis

A strong thesis must know its limits, or it becomes a tautology.

  1. Structure does not explain why there is something rather than nothing.
  2. It does not explain consciousness in full. The hard problem remains.
  3. It does not eliminate contingency and randomness. Structure shapes probability distributions; it does not eliminate chance.
  4. It is not the only level of explanation. History, intention, phenomenology also matter. Structure explains constraints and incentives, not the choice itself.
  5. It risks triviality if “structure” means everything. It is powerful only when defined precisely and specific structural changes are linked to specific behavioral changes.

The thesis is a lens, a method, a way of seeing, not a theory of everything.

Section XV

The distinction between structure and order

Structure is the arrangement itself. Order is the degree to which that arrangement is regular, predictable, low in entropy. A structure can be highly ordered (crystal, well-designed factory, formal proof), moderately ordered (living cell, city, language), or disordered (gas, chaotic system, random pile).

Order is a property of structure. Life is structure that maintains order against entropy by consuming energy and exporting disorder. Intelligence is structure that can model order, predict order, create order. Design is the intentional creation of order that serves a purpose.

The refined phrase

Everything is structure. Order is a property of structure that determines predictability and function. Entropy is the tendency of structure to decay into disorder unless maintained by flows of energy and information.

Section XVI

The temporal dimension: structure changes over time

Three processes that change structure

Growth: adding components and connections. Differentiation: components specialize. Reorganization: changing arrangement, connections, rules.

Three processes that destroy structure

Entropy: physical decay. Disruption: external shocks breaking connections. Obsolescence: the adaptive becomes maladaptive when the environment changes.

The history of life, technology, and civilization is structures being created, maintained, adapted, replaced. Innovation is the creation of a new structure better adapted to a new environment, goal, or possibility.

Synthesis

Fourteen points, in one place

  1. Reality is structured at every scale.
  2. A structure is defined by components, arrangement, connections, flows, rules.
  3. Behavior emerges from structure and relationships, not parts in isolation.
  4. Information is structure that can be read by another structure.
  5. Knowledge is structured information that allows prediction.
  6. Design is the intentional shaping of structure to shape possibility.
  7. Emergent design is shaping by selection over time without a central designer.
  8. To change what is possible, change the structure.
  9. To change behavior durably, change the structure, not the exhortation.
  10. Good design makes the right things easy and the wrong things hard.
  11. Structure determines behavior, function, purpose, meaning, value, identity, existence.
  12. Order is a property of structure, entropy its decay; life and intelligence are processes that create and maintain order.
  13. The ultimate test is empirical: a better outcome without violating constraints that genuinely matter.
  14. The ultimate art is a structure that is functional, coherent, adaptable, elegant, where elegance is a sign the structure is well-aligned with its purpose.

And imagination allows us to conceive what comes next.

Closing · Figure 5

The compressed thesis

You are not arguing for the rejection of structure. You are arguing for the intelligent interrogation of structure. Understand not only how something works but why it works that way, which conditions make that arrangement necessary, which assumptions make it appear necessary, and what other arrangements become possible when those assumptions are reconsidered.

The ultimate test is practical: can the new arrangement produce a demonstrably better outcome without violating the constraints that genuinely matter? If yes, the idea has progressed from speculation to engineering. When engineering produces a structure that is functional, coherent, adaptable, and elegant, the art becomes visible in the result.
