Draft on disk, unpublished.

1/ Most agent teams are designed backwards. People name roles first: a research agent, a review agent, a debate coordinator. Roles are premature. Start with evidence needs. What facts must be established? That is falsifiable. Roles are not.

2/ The rule for mapping evidence needs to capabilities: cheapest first. A database lookup beats a reasoning chain when the fact is in the database. Retrieval beats generation. Deterministic tools beat model inference. Most teams pay model prices for lookup work.

3/ Then compile the sparsest graph that respects dependency order. Parallel where independent. Sequential where dependent. Human-gated where authority requires it. That is the whole supervisor-worker topology. Anything extra has to earn its place or it is decoration.

4/ No all-to-all by default. Every agent talking to every agent multiplies cost and spreads noise: one agent's hallucinated confidence becomes another agent's cited fact becomes the final report's asserted finding. Sparse graphs are cheaper and, at scale, more reliable.

5/ Put authority in the graph, not the dashboard. Every node carries a declared ceiling: observe, recommend, draft, stage, execute. What runs is the intersection of what the planner proposed, what policy permits, and what infrastructure can provide right now.

6/ When an edge does not survive that intersection, record it. Blocked by policy or unavailable, with the reason and the rule cited. The absence of the edge is evidence. Systems that go quiet on the degraded path are the ones that produce the incidents.

7/ Self-approval is not governance. If the proposing agent is also the approving agent, you have one set, not three, and the intersection is theater. And a human on every step is not the claim. Human approvals are declared nodes at declared gates. One edge, not a hover.

8/ A receipt is the org chart that did the work, kept as a first-class record. Nodes, edges, authority per node, reductions, round-level changes, cost per node, outcome, unsupported claims counted. A hash without a story is a souvenir.

9/ Define "consequential" before you instrument. Any run that emits an artifact, spends over a threshold, touches production data, or acts above observe authority gets a full receipt. Everything else gets a light receipt. Coverage of consequences, not paperwork.

10/ Then ablate. Remove the debate and run one reasoning pass over the same evidence. If quality holds, the debate was theater. Measure your reviewer agent's intervention rate. A reviewer that never blocks anything in a hundred runs is latency wearing an authority costume.

11/ The monthly review is the highest-leverage meeting you are not having. Three outputs: configurations promoted on evidence, configurations killed on evidence, schema changes. It is a court and the receipts are the exhibits. Nothing moves on narrative.

12/ You do not need a new system to start. You need a receipt schema, one consequential workflow, and the monthly review. The company that learns which organizations work, fastest, wins. Adapted from "The Org Chart Is the Product" (The Neuruh Papers). More at neuruh.com.
