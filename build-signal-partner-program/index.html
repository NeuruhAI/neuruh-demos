The liquidity curve calculator

Transition liquidity: the $329,000 pool

Handoff sections 11 and 12. The key insight: the customer may already possess sufficient economic value to solve the problem. The friction is timing. The filing's example household owns a $275,000 house, an $18,000 vehicle, $10,000 tools, $8,000 collectibles, $6,000 jewelry, and $12,000 equipment, plus household estate inventory, but needs $20,000 now to move, clean, repair, stage, downsize, and pay transition expenses. Traditional system sees a borrower asking for money. Neuruh sees a $329,000+ fragmented asset pool requiring $20,000 of temporary liquidity to unlock orderly disposition.

The transition liquidity thesis (section 12): we are not primarily underwriting the consumer. We are underwriting the asset pool and its liquidation pathways. The system evaluates asset coverage, valuation confidence, liquidity depth, liquidation timeline, execution control, capital required, coverage ratio, downside recovery, and customer outcome.

The capital underwriting formula

Advance Capacity = Conservative Liquidation Value x Eligible Advance Rate - Execution Costs - Risk Reserve

Not all assets receive equal advance rates. The filing's illustrative concept: gold high liquidity; common vehicles relatively high; equipment medium-high; graded collectibles medium; raw collectibles lower; household contents lower; unique antiques low unless buyer commitment exists; real estate structure-specific. The AI should not simply say "$100,000 value." It should say: "$100,000 estimated market value, $76,000 conservative liquidation value, $62,000 highly executable 14-day liquidity." Farm Continuity Liquidity and Heirloom Asset Recovery

Farm Continuity Liquidity (sections 24 and 25): aging farmers, no clear successor, family does not want to operate the farm, significant land and equipment value, limited liquid capital, operational burden increasing, farm may eventually be sold piecemeal. The business is not merely buying farms: provide liquidity, succession intelligence, transition support, and alternative ownership or operating structures that preserve productive American agricultural assets while allowing aging owners to access value. Possible structures to investigate include sale-leaseback, partial land acquisition, long-term lease, operating partnership, farm-management transition, equity extraction, equipment monetization, conservation structure, family succession structure, land trust partnerships, farmer-to-younger-farmer transfer, buy-and-lease-to-operator, agricultural SPV, community or strategic ownership structures, development rights transactions, and partial acreage monetization. Mission language: "Preserve productive American land while giving aging owners fair access to the value they built."

Heirloom Asset Recovery (sections 26 to 29): older farms, orchards, homesteads, nurseries, and rural properties may contain historically valuable biological assets that have disappeared from commercial markets: heritage apple varieties, old pear varieties, heritage grapes, historic berries, nut trees, old rootstocks, seed varieties, heritage livestock genetics, historic garden plants, regional cultivars, old orchard genetics. AI could help rediscover them from historical newspapers, agricultural extension documents, county histories, old farm advertisements, nursery catalogs, plat maps, historic aerial imagery, farm journals, agricultural census records, university archives, fruit-growing association records, local historical societies, genealogy records, oral histories, auction records, property ownership history, USDA documents, seed catalogs, and orchard maps. Example in the filing: AI finds a 1913 article about the Smith farm outside X Township, known for Northern Spy, Spitzenburg, and several locally developed apple varieties, then identifies the historic location, present parcels, current landowners, surviving orchard pattern from aerial imagery, likelihood of old trees, and family descendants. Preservation credibility should come before aggressive commercialization.

How the conversation ran

A voice-mode conversation, 17 user turns and 17 assistant turns, Sat Aug 22 at 8:58 AM to Sun Aug 23 at 3:13 AM. The arc, in the filing's own order:

Turns 1 to 3: The signal economy

Jeramie asks how to turn the gig market into an opportunity market by pitching services to tree services and other first responders. The answer: build a Signal Partner Program. Treat tree services, roofers, plumbers, and junk haulers as sensors out in the world. They see distress first; a simple mobile form captures address, event type, photos, and permission; AI enriches, scores, and routes. Partners become field originators who own the first touch while Neuruh runs the back office. Static data is dead inventory unless something interprets change.

Turns 4 to 9: The three layers

Transition liquidity capital: people are asset rich, cash poor, stuck. Layer two is personal asset liquidity: the customer sits on an asset stack (cards, collectibles, gold, vehicles, tools, equipment, estate contents) and AI values and routes it. "You don't need to be a lender. You need to be a router." Layer three is transition liquidity: moving, downsizing, aging, assisted living, estate cleanout, business closure, retirement. That is where the most value is and where the most friction is. Turns 4, 8, and 9 are cut off mid-line in the browser view.

Turns 10 to 13: Bridge capital and investor language

Bridge financing, bridge capital, liquidity capital, transition capital: capital to fund the deals and the liquidity behind them. The investor pitch is reframed: do not pitch "we need money to go do deals." Pitch origination and rights: "we control the sourcing and routing of off-market asset liquidity. Capital plugs into that." Turn 13 is cut off mid-line in the browser view.

Turns 14 to 15: Farm Continuity Liquidity and Heirloom Asset Recovery

Older farmers who cannot continue, and the chance to preserve productive American land while giving aging owners access to value. Then old orchards and forgotten heirloom varieties: AI parses information, creates outreach, and tries to find and save what history lost. Turn 15 is cut off mid-line in the browser view.
