Thread 5: The Robot Question Is Insurance

Distills essay-5.md for @NeuruhAI. Every post under 280 chars (count noted). No emojis, hashtags, or em dashes. Every number audit-dated.

  1. In 2025 I wrote a paper arguing the robot question was never intelligence. It was insurance. Who pays when the robot crashes, and how do we prove what happened. 2026 ran the receipts on it. Here is what held and what I got wrong. (230)

  2. A chatbot hallucinates and an email has a wrong number in it. A 200-kilo humanoid hallucinates on a warehouse floor and you get a workers comp claim and an OSHA investigation. Same failure, different physics. One is a typo. One is a tort. (251)

  3. The 2026 scorecard. 60+ property and casualty groups filed AI exclusions (audit: July 2026). Regulators approved over 80% of exclusion filings from Berkshire, Chubb, Travelers, AIG subsidiaries (April 2026). The risk-pricers said: we cannot price this. (257)

  4. Sept 8, 2026: Hangzhou passed the first dedicated robot law in the world. Liability frameworks, insurance requirements, oversight ratios. Shenzhen, Shanghai, Beijing, Wuhan followed within 48 hours. Scale arrived first. The law is sprinting. (251)

  5. What my paper got wrong. China deployed faster than governance. I underweighted fleet correlation: one bad update, a thousand robots fail at once. And no neutral "DMV for robots" exists. That part is still a thesis, not an incumbent. (245)

  6. The fix has four moves. First: separate the planner from the authorizer. The system that wants to move never permits the move. Aviation figured this out. The pilot flies, traffic control clears the route. (209)

  7. Second: score every action for risk before it happens. An underwriter cannot price a fleet, but it can price a distribution of scored actions. Ten million scored actions is an actuarial object. Ten million unscored ones is a prayer. (232)

  8. Third: make insurance the execution gate. The action pays a micro-premium, gets a signed permit, then the motors unlock. Annual policies price the average. The average lies. Risk concentrates in specific actions and shifts. (226)

  9. Fourth: keep an append-only record. Proposal, score, premium, permit, outcome. When the arm hits the chassis, three vendors point at each other. The log ends the argument. Reading exercise, not a lawsuit. (218)

  10. Honesty, every time: the Insured Action Protocol (micro-premium per action, permit before the motors unlock) is a design proposal. No one has built it. No insurer has underwritten it. No regulator has blessed it. Blueprint, not building. (251)

  11. Novel governance is a sales objection. Boring compliance is a sales accelerator. Enterprise deals die in risk review, not technical review. Anchor to NIST and SOC 2. The CFO signs deals, not the engineer. (210)

  12. The future is not about the smartest chatbot. It is about the safest hands, and who can prove it. Start the record on day one. Storage is cheap. Reconstruction is expensive. That line closed my 2025 paper. Still true. (231)
