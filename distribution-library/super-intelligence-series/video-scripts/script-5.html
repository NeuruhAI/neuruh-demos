Video Script 5: The Robot Question Is Insurance

60-90 second talking head. One idea: liability, not intelligence, is the gate. Plain spoken language, conversational. ~180 words, about 70 seconds at normal pace.

[HOOK, first 3 seconds, look straight at camera]

Your robot is not going to fail because it is dumb. It is going to fail to deploy because nobody can insure it.

[Body]

In 2025 I wrote a paper called The Liability Layer. My argument was simple. The thing gating robots is not intelligence. It is liability. Who pays when the robot crashes, and how do we prove what happened.

Think about it this way. A chatbot hallucinates, you get a typo in an email. A 200-kilo humanoid hallucinates on a warehouse floor, you get a workers comp claim and an OSHA investigation. Same failure, different physics. One is a typo. One is a tort.

2026 ran the receipts. More than sixty property and casualty groups filed AI exclusions, audit date July 2026. The companies whose whole job is pricing risk looked at AI and walked away.

So the fix is not a smarter model. It is boring stuff. Separate the planner from the authorizer. Score the risk before the action. Keep a record nobody can rewrite. Buyers do not buy intelligence. They buy throughput that survives the first incident.

[Close]

That is Book 5 of The Neuruh Papers. The robot question is insurance. Safest hands win.
